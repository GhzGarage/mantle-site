This guide covers how to build each kind of wearable item in Unreal so it passes Mantle's checks and fits players' avatars. For opening the Mantle Cosmetics tab, signing in, the listing fields, Check only, Upload to Mantle and screening, see Making avatar items. Avatars themselves, emotes, scars and tattoos are in Avatars and emotes.

What you can make Live

In the Cosmetics tab, What are you publishing? sets the kind of item, and the box next to it sets its slot. Each slot holds one item at a time on an avatar.

3D clothing is only worn by avatars that take clothing: avatars built as a MetaHuman with Mantle's reference body (see Converting a character and How clothing fits every body). Hair is worn by converted avatars only. Accessories go on any avatar whose skeleton has the socket. The nails slot is for shaped press-on nails: it never hides or sinks the fingertips.

If you have a character from Fab or MetaHuman Creator, or another pack, that the checks refuse, Convert a character can refit a clothing or hair mesh from it for you. The section shows for Clothing and Hair as well as Avatar. If the character comes dressed, Convert can also turn its clothes into Outfits: see Splitting a clothed character.

3D clothing Live

A top, bottom or pair of shoes is a Skeletal Mesh skinned to the MetaHuman body skeleton (metahuman_base_skel). You make it on one MetaHuman body, and Mantle fits it to every other body that wears it.

  1. Start from a MetaHuman body

    Use a MetaHuman body that has Mantle's reference topology: an LOD with the same vertex layout as Mantle's reference body. Export the MetaHuman you'll dress with UE Optimized at Medium, High or Cinematic quality, without clothing. For a Cinematic MetaHuman from Fab, the body is SKM_<Name>_Body under CharacterAssemblies/<Name>/Body.

  2. Model and skin the garment on that body

    Skin it to the body's own skeleton, with the bones in the same place as the body's reference pose (within 0.5 cm and 1 degree). Use the MetaHuman bone names. Letter case doesn't matter. A vertex with more than 8 bone influences keeps its 8 heaviest, as described under Messages.

  3. Choose Clothing and the slot

    In What are you publishing? choose Clothing, then top, bottom, footwear or nails. Under Asset, pick the garment's Skeletal Mesh. Sections lists its material sections with the ones for this slot already ticked, going by their names (a section named like skin is grayed out and left unticked). Check the ticks and change them if they're wrong.

  4. Check Authored on

    Authored on is the MetaHuman body you made the garment on. The tab fills it in when it finds a suitable body in your project, including a Cinematic MetaHuman under /Game/CharacterAssemblies/; if it picked the wrong one, choose yours. This body uploads with the item. So does the MetaHuman face next to it, which Mantle uses to fit collars.

  5. Color it if you want

    Under Clothing colors, choose which areas players can recolor. See Clothing colors.

  6. Preview it

    Under Preview, try the garment on the body you made it on or on another avatar by its item id (you need to be signed in for that). Nothing is uploaded.

  7. Check only, then upload

    Choose Check only and fix anything marked "Problem". Then Upload to Mantle (see Making avatar items).

How clothing fits every body

  • Mantle fits the garment to each wearer's body, starting from the body it was Authored on. That's why the Authored on body must be a MetaHuman body with Mantle's reference topology, and why every bone that carries weight has to be bound in that body's reference pose.
  • The plugin adds everything the Authored on body needs when you upload, so there's nothing extra for you to do.
  • When Convert makes an Outfit from a clothed character, it shapes the MetaHuman's skin under the clothing to match the character's body. The clothing stays as you made it on that body, and Epic's resize takes it from there to each wearer's body, so parts such as a corset's cups aren't crushed on a flatter bust. See Splitting a clothed character.
  • Body skin is hidden only where a top, bottom or shoes fully covers it, so it can't poke through the garment. Skin that shows through a cutout or between straps stays visible.
  • Skin near an edge or opening stays visible: within 2.5 cm of a neckline, armhole, strap, hem or cutout (1.5 cm for shoes). That way there are no see-through gaps where the garment opens. Skin you can see is never shrunk or pushed in to fit the garment.
  • Hair doesn't hide anything.
  • Preview hides skin the same way players' games do, so you can check it before you upload. See Previewing.
  • Only avatars that take clothing wear 3D clothing. On other avatars, clothing and hair are set aside and go back on when the player switches to an avatar that fits them. They still wear accessories and emotes: that includes a UE5 Mannequin avatar and a MetaHuman exported another way.

One-piece outfits

When one mesh holds several garments, such as a jumpsuit or a character's full outfit, upload it once per slot. Choose the slot, tick only that piece's sections under Sections, upload, then do the same for the next slot. Files and details lists the sections each upload takes. The tab ticks the sections whose names fit the slot you chose, and sections that look like the character's own skin can't be ticked. If one is in the upload anyway, you'll see: "section "[name]" looks like the character's own skin, so every wearer would wear it. … Untick that section under Sections." If you tick sections of more than one garment for one slot, the tab says "this mesh holds more than one garment (…) and uploads as one "[slot]" item." With nothing ticked, it says "Problem: no section of [mesh] is chosen for this item."

Parametric Outfits Live

For a garment that should fit many body shapes, Clothing also takes a parametric Outfit (a MetaHuman Outfit Asset). You make one from your garment and the original body and head it was made on, then check it. Mantle fits it to Epic's standard body types for you. When you choose Clothing, the panel says: "Choose a skeletal garment or a parametric Outfit. Create an Outfit from the garment and its original body/head, then publish one clothing item. Sizes are made for you from a MetaHuman Outfit, a garment made on a MetaHuman, or a garment with its character's bare body beside it."

  1. Pick the garment

    Choose Clothing and pick the garment's Skeletal Mesh under Asset.

  2. Create the Outfit

    Under Parametric clothing, pick the original combined body and head the garment was made on: "Its shape, pose and coordinates must match the garment; a converted body alone is not this source." Then choose Create parametric Outfit. It makes an asset called OA_[garment name], picks it as the Asset for you and says "Saved [path]. Mantle fits it to Epic's standard bodies automatically; choose what it was made for, then check and upload it."

  3. Choose what it was made for

    Next to Made for:, choose Automatic, Women's body or Men's body. It's the style of body you made the clothing on, and it picks whether the sizes are built on Epic's standard women's or men's bodies. Automatic guesses from the Outfit's own sizes. The panel says: "Mantle fits the clothing to Epic's standard body types automatically (listed below), so nothing needs converting."

  4. Add your own fit source (optional)

    To fit a body of your own as well, pick a Creator character under Authored on. Its converted body and face are picked for you when the character has only one of each; otherwise choose them yourself. Then choose Add selected converted fit source. Mantle's 3 standard bodies count towards the limit of 8 in all, so you can add up to 5 of your own. The ones you add are named fit1, fit2 and so on. All of them upload as one clothing item. Epic's tools pick the right size for each source. Choose Remove to take one out: it works on the standard bodies too.

  5. Check and upload

    Choose Check only, then Upload to Mantle. If Mantle can't build its standard bodies for the Outfit, or you've removed every fit source, the check says "Mantle could not make its standard fit bodies for this Outfit, so add at least one fit source in Parametric clothing before checking or uploading it." followed by the reason, when there is one. Add a fit source of your own, as above.

The Preview section also has an Automatic source size (preview) box. It only changes the preview: "Uploads automatically select from every authored size for each fit source."

Sizes for a skeletal mesh garment Live

A Skeletal Mesh garment can upload in Epic's standard body sizes like a parametric Outfit. You don't have to make the Outfit yourself. Mantle tries these in order:

  1. Made on a converted body: the garment sits on a body Mantle converted from a MetaHuman Creator character. Mantle makes the garment into an Outfit directly. An exported MetaHuman body doesn't count.
  2. A bare character body next to it: Mantle makes the Outfit from that body. The body must be the whole character, at least about 120 cm tall. It needs a skin section, or a name like Body, Nude or Naked, and no clothing sections. It can't be a head or face mesh. It must also list the garment as one of its separate clothing meshes. Keep it in the garment's folder or one or two folders above. Mantle shapes the MetaHuman only under the garment to match this body, so a garment fits the same way as one Convert made for you. Your assets aren't changed. Mantle saves the Outfit as OA_[garment] in Converted/[Body]_Outfit next to the body, and reuses it next time. You'll see "The bare-skin step was confirmed automatically for [body]: it has no clothing sections and a skin section."
  3. Neither: the garment uploads in one size. You'll see "Uploads in one size: it could not be made in sizes ([why]).", for example "no MetaHuman authoring body was found for it; and no whole bare character body was found next to it".

When it works, the check says "Made as a MetaHuman Outfit ([route]), so it uploads in Epic's standard body sizes." To get sizes, put the character's whole bare body next to the garment and tick all of the garment's sections. Checking takes longer than before, because Mantle builds the fit bodies.

The Cosmetics tab takes a Skeletal Mesh or a MetaHuman Outfit. An AI agent can also give a Chaos cloth asset to CheckCosmetic and UploadCosmetic. A cloth asset gets sizes only by the first route.

Clothing colors Live

Players can recolor the areas of a garment you mark. Only tops, bottoms, shoes and accessories can be recolored. Mantle finds the areas on its own for all four.

The Clothing colors section only shows when you've chosen Clothing. So you can mark areas by hand only on tops, bottoms and shoes. Changing the kind of item clears them. If you declare areas on something that isn't clothing, the check says "Declared clothing colors are supported only on clothing items."

Recoloring changes color only. The normal and surface maps stay as you made them. Parts you don't mark keep their own colors.

  • Found for you: Mantle looks for the parts players can recolor when you check or upload, so you don't have to do anything. Choose Preview once to see them: "We found N areas players can recolour. Untick any you'd like to keep fixed." It uses the area maps in the material, which are textures named like a mask. If none marks out separate areas, the whole material becomes one area called "Main". If it finds nothing to recolor, it says "This item uploads in its original colours."
  • Show the areas: tick "Show each area in its own colour in the preview" to see them. Each area has a name box ("The name players see in the colour menu."), at most 64 characters.
  • Mark them yourself: open "Advanced: mark the areas yourself".
    1. Choose Set up areas on a material.
    2. Type the material's name. The list above the box shows them. For a parametric Outfit, preview once to fill the list.
    3. Pick your own Area texture. Red, green and blue each mark one area. Turn sRGB off and use one UV tile. Otherwise you'll see "Problem: A clothing colour mask must be a linear data texture with one UV tile. Disable sRGB on the explicitly authored RGB mask."
    4. Name each area and give it a default swatch.
    Anything you set up here replaces the areas Mantle found. Very dark areas need a lighter version of the item's color texture. Pick it under "Lighter colour texture (optional)". Preview colors don't change what uploads.

When Mantle finds the areas itself, it needs a map that's close to black and white with hard edges. It can't split a map that uses many colors as separate areas (an ID map). When you mark the areas yourself, neither applies. Your Area texture gives up to three areas, one each for red, green and blue.

An area with no color detail, such as pure black, keeps its original colors if Mantle found it. Files and details says why. If you marked it yourself, it's a Problem instead. If an item has more than 8 areas in all, the extra parts keep their original colors and the tab says so.

Skirts, dresses and long coats

A garment that hangs below the knee of the body it was made on can still be published. It may clip through the legs when the wearer moves, so the tab shows "Note: this garment hangs below the knee, so it may clip through the legs when the wearer moves. It can still be published: Mantle marks it "may clip in motion" so players know." After upload the result adds "It hangs below the knee, so Mantle marks it "may clip in motion": the legs can show through it when the wearer moves." Shoes are below the knee by nature and are never marked.

Hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial hair Live

Hair has four slots: hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial_hair (beards and moustaches). A player can wear one item in each, all at once. Every hair item follows the wearer's head. Grooms and cards meshes upload as hair cards; a section lifted from a Skeletal Mesh (such as eyelashes) uploads with its own material's textures.

  1. Choose Hair and the slot

    Choose Hair, then the slot.

  2. Pick the source under Asset

    It can be one of three things:

    • A groom: Mantle converts the groom's cards (Groom editor → Cards), not its strands, and uploads them with the hair's color, read from the groom's materials or set under Hair colour. A second box lists the groom's cards as "Group N, LOD N: [mesh] (N textures)". It starts on "Cards: automatic (finest LOD that fits the budget)". If the cards are over the slot's triangle limit, Mantle drops whole cards to fit and says so under Files and details; pick a lower-detail entry if you'd rather choose the detail yourself. If the groom has no cards, choose Generate cards from strands.
    • A hair cards Static Mesh: the tab looks for the cards' atlas textures in the mesh's materials, in a groom named after the mesh, or next to the mesh. Name them [name]_CardsAtlas_Attribute and [name]_CardsAtlas_Tangent. If it finds none, it uses the textures of the mesh's material. Mantle uses the first LOD you made that fits the slot's triangle limit. If none does, it drops whole cards to fit.
    • One section of a Skeletal Mesh: for example a MetaHuman face's eyelashes. A box asks you to pick the section, or "Whole hair mesh (all sections)". A section with "lash" in its name is picked for you. It's trimmed by whole cards in the same way.
  3. Check Authored on

    As with 3D clothing, Authored on is the MetaHuman body or head the hair was made on. It must have a head bone. The tab finds it from the groom's binding or the groom the cards came from, and asks you to choose when it can't tell. Hair also needs the Source MetaHuman face it was made on: "Leave empty to find the face beside the source body; select it explicitly for standalone cards." If the body is itself a MetaHuman head, it is the face. The face guides how the hair is fitted. A body shape isn't uploaded for head-fitted hair.

  4. Preview it

    Under Preview, try the hair on its source head or on a catalog avatar. Nothing is uploaded.

  5. Check only, then upload

Generate cards from strands Live

If you pick a strand groom, Generate cards from strands starts the installed Hair Card Generator, which opens its own settings window. The settings start at 500 cards, the slot's triangle limit and a 2,048-pixel atlas. Change them there if you like. After it finishes, the tab says "Cards generated. Check the item and preview their placement before uploading." and runs the check. Review the result before you publish.

The button needs Epic's optional Hair Card Generator plugin (it's experimental and off by default) turned on in your project, with its Python setup done, and an interactive editor. The Mantle plugin turns on the other plugins it needs, but not this one, and it doesn't install anything for you. If it isn't available, the tab says so: "the optional Hair Card Generator is unavailable: enable it in this creator project and complete its Python setup, or use MetaHuman Optimized build cards; Mantle does not enable plugins or install dependencies".

Hair bought for another head Live

Hair cards you bought often weren't made for a MetaHuman. When you pick a hair cards Static Mesh like that, Mantle sizes and places it on the standard head for you. It works for short, long, braided and ponytail styles. Grooms and skeletal mesh sections are left as they are, and so are cards that come from a MetaHuman groom.

  1. Choose the hair and its face

    Pick the cards under Asset. For hair made for another head, pick any converted MetaHuman body under Authored on and its face under Source MetaHuman face, and Mantle fits the hair to that face. Then choose Check only.

  2. Read the fit card

    A card under the checks starts with the route's title and sentence (see Which route your hair takes), then says "We sized your hair to fit the standard head". Under that are two pictures side by side, drawn from the same three-quarter angle: "As you made it" and "Fitted to the standard head". Each shows the hair on a head, so you can compare them. If the pictures can't be drawn, the card shows without them. Any warnings show under the pictures. Open Preview too, and look closely at how the hair sits.

  3. Accept it or place it yourself

    Under the pictures and warnings are two buttons: Looks good and Adjust. Choose Looks good to keep the fit: the card changes to "Hair fit confirmed." Choose Adjust to upload the hair where you made it instead, so you can place it by hand. Hover over Adjust to read "Upload this hair where you made it instead, so you can place it by hand."

  4. Go back to the automatic fit

    After Adjust, the card keeps the route's title and sentence, then says "This hair uploads where you made it; it was not sized to the standard head." Choose Fit it for me to size it again.

The fit keeps hair off the face only where it put the hair there. Hair that already covered the face as you made it, such as a fringe or a lock over one eye, stays. If the fit pushed a lock onto the face, Mantle puts it back where the first sizing left it. Under Files and details, a note says "Hair over the face: [N] hair vertices that the fit had pushed onto the face were put back." It shows only when something was put back.

Some packs have a solid skullcap under the cards. Mantle finds it, splits it off and wraps it onto the head. Under Files and details, the cap notes start "Hair cap:". When Mantle finds a cap, the note reads "Hair cap: found [N] cap piece(s)[ in section [name]] among [N] pieces, [N] cap triangles; its open edge is wrapped onto the head." The section part shows only when the cap is in one named section. Without a cap, it reads "Hair cap: none found, so the hair is fitted as cards only." A big piece that hangs off the head is treated as hair, not a cap: "Hair cap: the big piece hangs [N] cm from the head, so it is treated as hair, not a cap." So are big pieces that don't spread over the top of the head, such as tails and braids: "Hair cap: the big pieces do not spread over the top of the head (tails or braids), so they are treated as hair, not a cap." Either of those two notes is followed by "Hair cap: none found, so the hair is fitted as cards only."

Hair from an OBJ file often comes in lying on its side. Mantle checks for that when it fits the hair. If the hair is lying down, it stands it upright for you, and under Files and details a note says "The hair was lying on its side (its file is Y-up), so it was turned upright." Hair that's already upright isn't touched.

The hair slot allows 14,000 triangles. A denser pack is thinned by dropping whole cards, and the cards that stay are spread evenly over the style, so the hair looks thin instead of patchy. Under Files and details, a note says "Whole-card budget reduction: [N] to [N] triangles (no card collapses)". Thinning never refuses the pack. Every section keeps at least its smallest card. If that's still over the limit, the hair keeps its smallest fit and the tab says "Note: this hair cannot be made smaller than [N] triangles without splitting a card (the limit is [limit]), so it keeps its smallest fit of at least one card from each section."

Accessories Live

An accessory is a rigid piece, normally a Static Mesh, that attaches to one socket on the avatar's skeleton. It doesn't bend. You can also pick a MetaHuman Outfit under Asset: Mantle makes one rigid piece from it on the reference head and notes "Outfit source size: [size], as a rigid accessory." A player can wear one accessory per socket, so 8 at most.

  1. Set the pivot

    The mesh's pivot is where it attaches: model it so the pivot sits on the point that touches the body, near the socket. How far the mesh reaches from its pivot is limited (below). If the pivot is out of reach, for example a model exported with its pivot at the feet, Mantle can center the mesh on the socket for you when it's small enough for the slot. See the paragraph after the table.

  2. Use one material

    Merge the mesh's materials into one. The accessory uploads that material's base color texture and normal map, up to 1024 x 1024. If the tab says the mesh has no CPU copy of its vertices, turn on Allow CPU Access on the mesh.

  3. Choose Accessory and the slot

    Choose Accessory, pick the slot (its socket), and pick the Static Mesh under Asset. LOD 0 is uploaded, along with any lower LODs you authored (up to 3 in all). A lower LOD that has more triangles than the one before it, or breaks a limit, is left off with a "Note". If the mesh is over the triangle limit, see below.

  4. Offset it if you need to

    Under Attachment, tick Offset it from the socket to move it: Location (cm) from -50 to 50 on each axis, Rotation (degrees) from -180 to 180, and Scale from 0.5 to 2. The first time you choose Preview or Check only for a new mesh and slot, the tab lines it up with a MetaHuman's axes for you (this ticks the offset and fills in the rotation). That needs a MetaHuman body, so preview first or pick one under Authored on. Align to avatar axes does it again. The tab remembers your offset for each mesh and slot and brings it back next time.

  5. Preview it

    Choose Preview. The tab finds a MetaHuman body in your project to show it on; pick one under Authored on if it can't. You can also preview on a catalog avatar. Check that it clears the hair and face.

The sockets come with Mantle's supported skeletons. An avatar creator can move a socket for bodies whose proportions put a hat, glasses or backpack in the wrong place, under Socket offsets when uploading the avatar (see Accessories and sockets). Your accessory then follows the moved socket. On an avatar whose skeleton doesn't have the socket, the accessory is set aside.

Messages and how to fix them

The tab starts each line with "Problem:" (must be fixed), "Note:" (worth knowing, doesn't block) or "Done:" (what it did for you). [Brackets] show values that change. The messages every kind shares are in What the panel checks.

Mantle checks every file again after upload. If it refuses one, the tab shows "[file]: failed - Mantle's processing refused it: [reason]", usually repeating one of the checks above. Two refusals come only from Mantle:

  • "This body shape has no warp weights, which garments need to fit other bodies. Upload it again with the Mantle plugin, which adds them."
  • "The body shape this item was made on has no warp weights, so it cannot fit other bodies. Upload the item again with the Mantle plugin, which sends a body shape that has them."

A hair item with too many or too large textures is refused when the item is created, for example "Mantle refused the item: a hair item may use at most 4 textures; [texture] ([where it's used]) is the 5th". Use fewer textures, or smaller ones.

How your items show to players Live

Once an item passes screening, it appears in the Catalog on the players' Avatar screen, under "Clothing", "Hair" or "Accessories" and the slot's own filter.

  • Set aside: if a player switches to an avatar that can't wear clothing, their clothing and hair are marked "SET ASIDE". They stay owned and go back on when the player switches to an avatar they fit. The Avatar screen says, for example, "1 item is set aside: it doesn't fit this avatar, and goes back on with one it fits."
  • Fitting: the same 3D clothing item fits every avatar that takes clothing.
  • Colors: if you marked areas under Clothing colors, players pick colors for them in the "Clothing colors" menu.
