This guide covers how to build each kind of wearable item in Unreal so it passes Mantle's checks and fits players' avatars. For opening the Mantle Cosmetics tab, signing in, the listing fields, Check only, Upload to Mantle and screening, see Making avatar items. Avatars themselves, emotes, scars and tattoos are in Avatars and emotes.

What you can make Live

In the Cosmetics tab, What are you publishing? sets the kind of item, and the box next to it sets its slot. Each slot holds one item at a time on an avatar.

3D clothing is only worn by avatars that take clothing: avatars built as a MetaHuman with Mantle's reference body (see Converting a character and How clothing fits every body). Hair is worn by converted avatars only. Accessories go on any avatar whose skeleton has the socket. The nails slot is for shaped press-on nails: it never hides or sinks the fingertips.

If you have a character from Fab, Sketchfab or MetaHuman Creator that the checks refuse, Convert a character can refit a clothing or hair mesh from it for you. The section shows for Clothing and Hair as well as Avatar.

3D clothing Live

A top, bottom or pair of shoes is a Skeletal Mesh skinned to the MetaHuman body skeleton (metahuman_base_skel). You make it on one MetaHuman body, and Mantle fits it to every other body that wears it.

  1. Start from a MetaHuman body

    Use a MetaHuman body that has Mantle's reference topology: an LOD with the same vertex layout as Mantle's reference body. Export the MetaHuman you'll dress with UE Optimized at Medium, High or Cinematic quality, without clothing. For a Cinematic MetaHuman from Fab, the body is SKM_<Name>_Body under CharacterAssemblies/<Name>/Body.

  2. Model and skin the garment on that body

    Skin it to the body's own skeleton, with the bones in the same place as the body's reference pose (within 0.5 cm and 1 degree). Use the MetaHuman bone names. Letter case doesn't matter. A vertex with more than 8 bone influences keeps its 8 heaviest, as described under Messages.

  3. Choose Clothing and the slot

    In What are you publishing? choose Clothing, then top, bottom, footwear or nails. Under Asset, pick the garment's Skeletal Mesh. Sections lists its material sections with the ones for this slot already ticked, going by their names (a section named like skin is greyed out and left unticked). Check the ticks and change them if they're wrong.

  4. Check Authored on

    Authored on is the MetaHuman body you made the garment on. The tab fills it in when it finds a suitable body in your project, including a Cinematic MetaHuman under /Game/CharacterAssemblies/; if it picked the wrong one, choose yours. This body uploads with the item as its body shape, together with the MetaHuman face found next to it, which is used to fit collars.

  5. Colour it if you want

    Under Clothing colors, choose which areas players can recolour. See Clothing colours.

  6. Preview it

    Under Preview, try the garment on the body you made it on or on another avatar by its item id (you need to be signed in for that). Nothing is uploaded.

  7. Check only, then upload

    Choose Check only and fix anything marked "Problem". Then Upload to Mantle (see Making avatar items).

How clothing fits every body

  • Mantle fits the garment to each wearer's body, starting from the body it was Authored on. That's why the Authored on body must be a MetaHuman body with Mantle's reference topology, and why every bone that carries weight has to be bound in that body's reference pose.
  • The plugin adds everything the Authored on body needs for fitting when you upload, so there's nothing extra for you to do. The fitting data is a set of 750 sample points (1,500 at most).
  • Body skin under a top, bottom or shoes is hidden, so it can't poke through the garment. Hair doesn't hide anything.
  • Only avatars that take clothing wear 3D clothing. On other avatars, clothing and hair are set aside and go back on when the player switches to an avatar that fits them. They still wear accessories and emotes: that includes a UE5 Mannequin avatar and a MetaHuman exported another way.

One-piece outfits

When one mesh holds several garments, such as a jumpsuit or a character's full outfit, upload it once per slot. Choose the slot, tick only that piece's sections under Sections, upload, then do the same for the next slot. Files and details lists the sections each upload takes. The tab ticks the sections whose names fit the slot you chose, and sections that look like the character's own skin can't be ticked. If one is in the upload anyway, you'll see: "section "[name]" looks like the character's own skin, so every wearer would wear it. … Untick that section under Sections." If you tick sections of more than one garment for one slot, the tab says "this mesh holds more than one garment (…) and uploads as one "[slot]" item." With nothing ticked, it says "Problem: no section of [mesh] is chosen for this item."

Parametric Outfits Live

For a garment that should fit many body shapes, Clothing also takes a parametric Outfit (a MetaHuman Outfit Asset). You make one from your garment and the original body and head it was made on, then check it. Mantle fits it to Epic's standard body types for you.

  1. Pick the garment

    Choose Clothing and pick the garment's Skeletal Mesh under Asset.

  2. Create the Outfit

    Under Parametric clothing, pick the original combined body and head the garment was made on: "Its shape, pose and coordinates must match the garment; a converted body alone is not this source." Then choose Create parametric Outfit. It makes an asset called OA_[garment name], picks it as the Asset for you and says "Saved [path]. Mantle fits it to Epic's standard bodies automatically; choose what it was made for, then check and upload it."

  3. Choose what it was made for

    Next to Made for:, choose Automatic, Women's body or Men's body. It's the style of body you made the clothing on, and it picks whether the sizes are built on Epic's standard women's or men's bodies. Automatic guesses from the Outfit's own sizes. The panel says: "Mantle fits the clothing to Epic's standard body types automatically (listed below), so nothing needs converting."

  4. Add your own fit source (optional)

    To fit a body of your own as well, pick a Creator character under Authored on. Its converted body and face are picked for you when the character has only one of each; otherwise choose them yourself. Then choose Add selected converted fit source. You can have up to 8. The ones you add are named fit1, fit2 and so on. All of them upload as one clothing item, and Epic chooses the authored size for each source. Choose Remove to take one out.

  5. Check and upload

    Choose Check only, then Upload to Mantle. If Mantle can't build its standard bodies for the Outfit, the check says "Mantle could not make its standard fit bodies for this Outfit, so add at least one fit source in Parametric clothing before checking or uploading it." followed by the reason. Add a fit source of your own, as above.

The Preview section also has an Automatic source size (preview) box. It only changes the preview: "Uploads automatically select from every authored size for each fit source."

Clothing colours Live

Players can recolour the areas of a garment you mark. The Clothing colors section only shows when you've chosen Clothing, and you can only mark areas by hand on clothing items. Mantle also finds areas on its own for tops, bottoms, shoes and accessories. If you mark areas on anything but clothing, the check says "Declared clothing colors are supported only on clothing items."" The recolouring changes colour only: the normal and surface maps stay as you made them, and parts you don't mark keep their own colours.

  • Found for you: Mantle looks for the parts players can recolour when you check or upload, so you don't have to do anything. Choose Preview once to see them: "We found N areas players can recolour. Untick any you'd like to keep fixed." It reads the area maps in the material (a texture named like a mask). If none marks out separate areas, the whole material becomes one area called "Main". If there are none at all, it says "This item uploads in its original colours."
  • Show the areas: tick "Show each area in its own colour in the preview" to see them. Each area has a name box ("The name players see in the colour menu."), at most 64 characters.
  • Mark them yourself: open "Advanced: mark the areas yourself". Choose Set up areas on a material, type the material's name (the list above it shows them; for a parametric Outfit, preview once to fill the list), pick your own Area texture (red, green and blue each mark one area) with sRGB turned off and one UV tile: otherwise you'll see "Problem: A clothing color mask must be a linear data texture with one UV tile. Disable sRGB on the explicitly authored RGB mask.", name each area, and give it a default swatch. Anything you set up here replaces the areas Mantle found. Very dark areas need a lighter version of the item's colour texture, which you pick under "Lighter colour texture (optional)". Preview colours don't change what uploads.

When Mantle finds the areas itself, the colour map has to be near black and white with hard edges, and a map that uses many colours as separate areas (an ID map) isn't split. When you mark the areas yourself, your Area texture uses red, green and blue as up to three separate areas, so neither rule applies. An area with no colour detail to work from, such as pure black, keeps its original colours when Mantle found it, and Files and details says why. For an area you marked yourself it's a Problem instead. If an item has more than 8 areas in all, the extra parts keep their original colours and the tab says so.

Skirts, dresses and long coats

A garment that hangs below the knee of the body it was made on can still be published. It may clip through the legs when the wearer moves, so the tab shows "Note: this garment hangs below the knee, so it may clip through the legs when the wearer moves. It can still be published: Mantle marks it "may clip in motion" so players know." After upload the result adds "It hangs below the knee, so Mantle marks it "may clip in motion": the legs can show through it when the wearer moves." Shoes are below the knee by nature and are never marked.

Hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial hair Live

Hair has four slots: hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial_hair (beards and moustaches). A player can wear one item in each, all at once. Every hair item follows the wearer's head. Grooms and cards meshes upload as hair cards; a section lifted from a Skeletal Mesh (such as eyelashes) uploads with its own material's textures.

  1. Choose Hair and the slot

    Choose Hair, then the slot.

  2. Pick the source under Asset

    It can be one of three things:

    • A groom: Mantle converts the groom's cards (Groom editor → Cards), not its strands, and uploads them with the hair's colour, read from the groom's materials or set under Hair colour. A second box lists the groom's cards as "Group N, LOD N: [mesh] (N textures)". It starts on "Cards: automatic (finest LOD that fits the budget)". If the cards are over the slot's triangle limit, Mantle drops whole cards to fit and says so under Files and details; pick a lower-detail entry if you'd rather choose the detail yourself. If the groom has no cards, choose Generate cards from strands.
    • A hair cards Static Mesh: the tab looks for the cards' atlas textures through the mesh's materials or the groom named after the mesh, or next to the mesh by name ([name]_CardsAtlas_Attribute and [name]_CardsAtlas_Tangent). If it finds none, it uses the textures of the mesh's material. Mantle takes the first authored LOD that fits the slot's triangle limit and, if none does, drops whole cards to fit.
    • One section of a Skeletal Mesh: for example a MetaHuman face's eyelashes. A box asks you to pick the section, or "Whole hair mesh (all sections)". A section with "lash" in its name is picked for you. It's trimmed by whole cards in the same way.
  3. Check Authored on

    As with 3D clothing, Authored on is the MetaHuman body or head the hair was made on. It must have a head bone. The tab finds it from the groom's binding or the groom the cards came from, and asks you to choose when it can't tell. Hair also needs the Source MetaHuman face it was made on: "Leave empty to find the face beside the source body; select it explicitly for standalone cards." If the body is itself a MetaHuman head, it is the face. The face guides how the hair is fitted. A body shape isn't uploaded for head-fitted hair.

  4. Preview it

    Under Preview, try the hair on its source head or on a catalog avatar. Nothing is uploaded.

  5. Check only, then upload

Generate cards from strands

If you pick a strand groom, Generate cards from strands starts the installed Hair Card Generator, which opens its own settings window. The settings start at 500 cards, the slot's triangle limit and a 2,048-pixel atlas, in one settings group for every card group. Change them there if you like. After it finishes, the tab says "Cards generated. Check the item and preview their placement before uploading." and runs the check. Review the result before you publish.

The button needs Epic's optional Hair Card Generator plugin (it's experimental and off by default) turned on in your project, with its Python setup done, and an interactive editor. The Mantle plugin turns on the other plugins it needs, but not this one, and it doesn't install anything for you. If it isn't available, the tab says so: "the optional Hair Card Generator is unavailable: enable it in this creator project and complete its Python setup, or use MetaHuman Optimized build cards; Mantle does not enable plugins or install dependencies".

Accessories Live

An accessory is a rigid Static Mesh that attaches to one socket on the avatar's skeleton. It doesn't bend. A player can wear one accessory per socket, so 8 at most.

  1. Set the pivot

    The mesh's pivot is where it attaches: model it so the pivot sits on the point that touches the body, near the socket. How far the mesh reaches from its pivot is limited (below).

  2. Use one material

    Merge the mesh's materials into one. The accessory uploads that material's base colour texture and normal map, up to 1024 x 1024. If the tab says the mesh has no CPU copy of its vertices, turn on Allow CPU Access on the mesh.

  3. Choose Accessory and the slot

    Choose Accessory, pick the slot (its socket), and pick the Static Mesh under Asset. LOD 0 is uploaded, along with any lower LODs you authored (up to 3 in all). A lower LOD that has more triangles than the one before it, or breaks a limit, is left off with a "Note". If the mesh is over the triangle limit, see below.

  4. Offset it if you need to

    Under Attachment, tick Offset it from the socket to move it: Location (cm) from -50 to 50 on each axis, Rotation (degrees) from -180 to 180, and Scale from 0.5 to 2. The first time you choose Preview or Check only for a new mesh and slot, the tab lines it up with a MetaHuman's axes for you (this ticks the offset and fills in the rotation). That needs a MetaHuman body, so preview first or pick one under Authored on. Align to avatar axes does it again. The tab remembers your offset for each mesh and slot and brings it back next time.

  5. Preview it

    Choose Preview. The tab finds a MetaHuman body in your project to show it on; pick one under Authored on if it can't. You can also preview on a catalog avatar. Check that it clears the hair and face.

"Longest side" is the longest edge of the mesh's bounding box. "Reach" is how far the box's farthest corner is from the pivot. Other limits: 4,000 triangles, 12,000 vertices, exactly 1 material. If the mesh has more than 4,000 triangles, the tab reduces a copy of it and notes "Accessory: reduced from N to N triangles to fit the 4000 limit." under Files and details, not in the check list. Your asset isn't changed. The copy has one LOD, so the LODs you authored are left off.

The sockets come with Mantle's supported skeletons. An avatar creator can move a socket for bodies whose proportions put a hat, glasses or backpack in the wrong place, under Socket offsets when uploading the avatar (see Accessories and sockets). Your accessory then follows the moved socket. On an avatar whose skeleton doesn't have the socket, the accessory is set aside.

Mantle checks every file again after upload. If it refuses one, the tab shows "[file]: failed - Mantle's processing refused it: [reason]", usually repeating one of the checks above. Two refusals come only from Mantle:

  • "This body shape has no warp weights, which garments need to fit other bodies. Upload it again with the Mantle plugin, which adds them."
  • "The body shape this item was made on has no warp weights, so it cannot fit other bodies. Upload the item again with the Mantle plugin, which sends a body shape that has them."

A hair item with too many or too large textures is refused when the item is created, for example "Mantle refused the item: a hair item may use at most 4 textures; [texture] ([where it's used]) is the 5th". Use fewer textures, or smaller ones.

How your items show to players Live

Once an item passes screening, it appears in the Catalog on the players' Avatar screen, under "Clothing", "Hair" or "Accessories" and the slot's own filter.

  • Set aside: if a player switches to an avatar that can't wear clothing, their clothing and hair are marked "SET ASIDE". They stay owned and go back on when the player switches to an avatar they fit. The Avatar screen says, for example, "1 item is set aside: it doesn't fit this avatar, and goes back on with one it fits."
  • Fitting: the same 3D clothing item fits every avatar that takes clothing.
  • Colours: if you marked areas under Clothing colours, players pick colours for them in the "Clothing colors" menu.
