This guide covers how to build each kind of wearable item in Unreal so it passes Mantle's checks and fits players' avatars. For opening the Mantle Cosmetics tab, signing in, the listing fields, Check only, Upload to Mantle and screening, see Making avatar items. Avatars themselves and emotes are in Avatars and emotes.

What you can make In progress

In the Cosmetics tab, What are you publishing? sets the kind of item, and the box next to it sets its slot. Each slot holds one item at a time on an avatar.

3D clothing and hair are only worn by avatars that take clothing (MetaHuman bodies exported the right way, see How clothing fits every body). Accessories go on any avatar whose skeleton has the socket.

3D clothing In progress

A top, bottom or pair of shoes is a Skeletal Mesh skinned to the MetaHuman body skeleton (metahuman_base_skel). You make it on one MetaHuman body, and Mantle fits it to every other body that wears it.

  1. Start from a MetaHuman body

    Export the MetaHuman you'll dress with UE Optimized at Medium, High or Cinematic quality, without clothing. For a Cinematic MetaHuman from Fab, the body is SKM_<Name>_Body under CharacterAssemblies/<Name>/Body. Garments made on a body exported this way fit other bodies.

  2. Model and skin the garment on that body

    Skin it to the body's own skeleton, with the bones in the same place as the body's reference pose. Use the MetaHuman bone names (pelvis, spine_01 to spine_05, clavicle_l, upperarm_l, thigh_l, calf_l, foot_l and so on, plus the MetaHuman twist and corrective bones). Letter case doesn't matter. A vertex with more than 8 bone influences keeps its 8 heaviest, as described under Messages.

  3. Choose Clothing and the slot

    In What are you publishing? choose Clothing, then top, bottom or footwear. Under Asset, pick the garment's Skeletal Mesh, and under Sections, tick the material sections that make up this garment.

  4. Check Authored on

    Authored on is the MetaHuman body you made the garment on. The tab fills it in when it finds a suitable body in your project, including a Cinematic MetaHuman under /Game/CharacterAssemblies/; if it picked the wrong one, choose yours. This body uploads with the item as its body shape, together with the MetaHuman face found next to it, which is used to fit collars.

  5. Preview it

    Under Preview, try the garment on one of Mantle's base avatars, on the body you made it on, or on another avatar by its item id. Nothing is uploaded.

  6. Check only, then upload

    Choose Check only and fix anything marked "Problem". Then Upload to Mantle (see Making avatar items).

How clothing fits every body

  • Mantle fits the garment to each wearer's body, starting from the body it was Authored on. That's why the Authored on body must be a MetaHuman body exported the right way, and why every bone that carries weight has to be bound in that body's reference pose.
  • The plugin adds everything the Authored on body needs for fitting when you upload, so there's nothing extra for you to do.
  • Body skin under a top, bottom or shoes is hidden, so it can't poke through the garment. Hair doesn't hide anything.
  • Only avatars that take clothing wear 3D clothing: MetaHuman bodies exported with UE Optimized at Medium, High or Cinematic quality, without clothing. UE5 Mannequin avatars and MetaHumans exported another way wear accessories and emotes only.

One-piece outfits

When one mesh holds several garments, such as a jumpsuit or a character's full outfit, upload it once per slot. Choose the slot, tick only that piece's sections under Sections, upload, then do the same for the next slot. Files and details lists the sections each upload takes. Sections that look like the character's own skin can't be part of a garment: "section "[name]" looks like the character's own skin, so every wearer would wear it. … Untick that section under Sections." If you tick sections of more than one garment for one slot, the tab says "this mesh holds more than one garment (…) and uploads as one "[slot]" item." With nothing ticked, it says "Problem: no section of [mesh] is chosen for this item."

Skirts, dresses and long coats

A garment that hangs below the knee of the body it was made on can still be published. It may clip through the legs when the wearer moves, so the tab shows "Note: this garment hangs below the knee, so it may clip through the legs when the wearer moves. It can still be published: Mantle marks it "may clip in motion" so players know." After upload the result adds "It hangs below the knee, so Mantle marks it "may clip in motion": the legs can show through it when the wearer moves." Shoes are below the knee by nature and are never marked.

Hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial hair In progress

Hair has four slots: hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial_hair (beards and moustaches). A player can wear one item in each, all at once. Every hair item is built from hair cards and follows the wearer's head.

  1. Choose Hair and the slot

    Choose Hair, then the slot.

  2. Pick the source under Asset

    It can be one of three things:

    • A groom: Mantle converts the groom's cards (Groom editor → Cards), not its strands, and uploads them with the hair's colour, read from the groom's materials or set under Hair colour. A second box lists the groom's cards as "Group N, LOD N: [mesh] (N textures)". By default it picks the LOD MetaHuman draws at Medium. Pick a lower-detail entry if the cards are over budget. MetaHuman grooms exported at Medium have cards.
    • A hair cards Static Mesh: keep the groom's cards atlas textures in the same folder as the cards mesh, so the tab finds them. Otherwise it uses the textures of the mesh's material.
    • One section of a Skeletal Mesh: for example a MetaHuman face's eyelashes. A box asks you to "Choose the section to lift (for example the eyelashes)".
  3. Check Authored on

    As with 3D clothing, Authored on is the MetaHuman body the hair was made on. It must have a head bone.

  4. Check only, then upload

Like clothing, hair is only worn by avatars that take clothing. In game, hair is drawn masked: a separate opacity texture is packed into the base colour's alpha, or the base colour's own alpha is used. Translucent hair gets "its material is translucent; Mantle draws hair masked (the alpha dithered, then smoothed over frames), so soft edges look a little harder."

Accessories In progress

An accessory is a rigid Static Mesh that attaches to one socket on the avatar's skeleton. It doesn't bend. A player can wear one accessory per socket, so 8 at most.

  1. Set the pivot

    The mesh's pivot is where it attaches: model it so the pivot sits on the point that touches the body, near the socket. How far the mesh reaches from its pivot is limited (below).

  2. Use one material

    Merge the mesh's materials into one. The accessory uploads that material's base colour texture, up to 1024 x 1024. If the tab says the mesh has no CPU copy of its vertices, turn on Allow CPU Access on the mesh.

  3. Choose Accessory and the slot

    Choose Accessory, pick the slot (its socket), and pick the Static Mesh under Asset. LOD 0 is uploaded.

  4. Offset it if you need to

    Under Attachment, tick Offset it from the socket to move it: Location (cm) from -50 to 50 on each axis, Rotation (degrees) from -180 to 180, and Scale from 0.5 to 2.

The sockets come with Mantle's supported skeletons (MetaHuman and UE5 Mannequin). An avatar creator can move a socket for bodies whose proportions put a hat, glasses or backpack in the wrong place, under Socket offsets when uploading the avatar (see Avatars and emotes). Your accessory then follows the moved socket. On an avatar whose skeleton doesn't have the socket, the accessory is set aside.

Mantle checks every file again after upload. If it refuses one, the tab shows "[file]: Mantle's processing refused it: [reason]", usually repeating one of the checks above. Two refusals come only from Mantle:

  • "This body shape has no warp weights, which garments need to fit other bodies. Upload it again with the Mantle plugin, which adds them."
  • "The body shape this item was made on has no warp weights, so it cannot fit other bodies. Upload the item again with the Mantle plugin, which sends a body shape that has them."

A hair item with too many or too large textures is refused when the item is created, for example "Mantle refused the item: a hair item may use at most 4 textures; [texture] (materials) is the 5th (materials)". Use fewer textures, or smaller ones.

How your items show to players In progress

Once an item passes screening, it appears in the Catalog on the players' Avatar screen, under "Clothing", "Hair" or "Accessories" and the slot's own filter.

  • Fits my avatar: this filter is on by default. While a player's avatar can't wear clothing, 3D clothing and hair are hidden from the Catalog. Accessories are always listed.
  • SET ASIDE: if a player switches to an avatar that can't wear clothing, their clothing and hair are marked "SET ASIDE". They stay owned and go back on when the player switches to an avatar they fit. The Avatar screen says, for example, "1 item is set aside: it doesn't fit this avatar, and goes back on with one it fits."
  • Fitting: the same 3D clothing item fits every avatar that takes clothing.
