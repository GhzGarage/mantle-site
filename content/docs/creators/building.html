Players get your world as you built it, using Mantle's built-in assets plus any meshes, materials, textures, sounds and animations from your project, so you can only publish the object types below.

Actor names and tags are kept, and actors set to Movable stay dynamic. There's no visibility setting, so a hidden actor on its own still shows up in the published world. Inside a Blueprint prefab, a part that's hidden in game is left out. Foliage and instanced meshes are left out only when they're hidden and have no collision.

If something is standing on a Player Start when a player spawns, for example a parked car or a physics prop, the player is placed on the nearest clear spot with ground under it, up to 8 m away. If nothing within 8 m is clear, the player is placed on the start anyway. Keep your Player Starts clear of Vehicle Spawns and anything else that moves.

What happens when you publish

  • Left out with a warning: BSP brushes, engine volumes (such as Trigger Volume and Blocking Volume), placed pawns and cameras (Mantle runs every player's camera itself). Use static meshes instead, and use Trigger Box or Trigger Sphere actors for trigger areas.
  • Left out with a warning: actors that only matter in the editor, such as camera rigs, level sequences, Notes and Target Points (nothing to change for these), and a few kinds of content a Fab map often has but Mantle has no place for. The rest of the place publishes without them. See Content that's left out for the list and what "Check this place" says.
  • Kept in part, with a warning: SkyAtmosphere, SkyLight, VolumetricCloud and ExponentialHeightFog ("Mantle provides the sky; this actor's settings are not published."), and Post Process Volumes. Mantle keeps some of their look and supplies the rest. See Your level's own look.
  • Left out with a warning: Blueprint logic, in actors and in the Level Blueprint: "Blueprint logic isn't published: the place gets this actor's components, not its behaviour." Use scripts for behavior.
  • Converted and uploaded: meshes, materials and sounds from your project, and your Script Assets. See Your own assets.
  • Uploaded with the place: the scripts in the level's Scripts folder, and the UI documents (.rml), stylesheets (.rcss) and images in its UI folder, next to Scripts. Images must end in .png or .jpg, in lower case (a .jpeg or .PNG file is an error: "must end in .png or .jpg, in lower case."). Files ending in .RML or .RCSS in capitals are an error too. Other files in UI are ignored. See UI documents. Script packages in the level's Packages folder are stored with the place too: see Script packages and Script Points.
  • Stops the publish with an error:
    • Unsupported actors, and extra components on an actor Mantle knows by class (such as a Point Light with a mesh added).
    • Names, tags or material overrides over the limits below, and meshes over the mesh limits.
    • A zero scale, or a negative scale on anything but a static mesh or a decal. A static mesh with a negative scale is published as a mirrored copy.
    • UI files that break the UI document limits, and an empty level.
    • A place that is too big: see Big levels.
    • On a World Partition level, Tools → Mantle → Check this place also stops until you've loaded every cell, but Publish loads them for you.
    See What "Check this place" catches.

Content that's left out Live

Fab maps often have actors Mantle has no place for. They don't stop your publish: each is left out, and "Check this place" tells you with a warning. The rest of the place publishes without them. "Check what players receive" lists them as dropped.

If you seeWhat it means
"is a camera rig (CameraRig_Rail), which only matters in the editor, so it is left out of the place."Nothing to change. The same message names a camera rig (Rail or Crane), a level sequence, a template sequence, a note, a target point, a navigation link, a level bounds marker, a Lightmass portal and a runtime virtual texture volume. The name in brackets is the actor's class.
"has no mesh assigned, so there is nothing to show and it is left out of the place."A Static Mesh Actor with no mesh. Pick a mesh for it to keep it.
"is a particle effect (NiagaraActor) whose particles Mantle can't carry yet (a Niagara system), so it is left out of the place."A placed Niagara Actor, or an Emitter Mantle can't translate. Fine if players won't miss it. Otherwise use a Mantle Effect actor. An Emitter with a Cascade system Mantle can translate publishes as Sprites effects instead, like the particle systems in a Blueprint prefab.
"uses Sound Cue '…', which plays no sound wave Mantle can publish, so it is left out of the place."An Ambient Sound whose Sound Cue plays no usable sound wave. Assign a sound wave imported from a WAV file to the Ambient Sound to keep it. See Sounds.
"has a scale of (0, 0, 0), so it covers nothing and is left out of the place."A Decal Actor with a zero scale. Give it a scale above zero on every axis to keep it. A decal with no usable material is left out the same way: see Decals.
"a sky mesh; Mantle supplies its own sky, so it isn't published."A Static Mesh Actor that is a level's own sky dome. See Your level's own look.
"is a Level Instance of '…': its N actors publish as ordinary items at their places in the level; the Level Instance itself is not kept."Nothing to change. A loaded Level Instance's actors publish on their own, and only the Level Instance itself is left out. This one shows in "Check what players receive". A Packed Level Actor isn't a Level Instance: it publishes as instanced meshes.
"is a Level Instance of '…' that isn't loaded in the editor, so what is inside it is left out of the place."Load it (right-click the Level Instance > Load) and publish again. Publish loads Level Instances for you, so you see this from Tools → Mantle → Check this place or when one won't load. See Big levels.

Big levels Live

Real Fab maps can be huge. Mantle checks how big your place will be before it converts or uploads anything, so a place that's too big stops right away, with the fix.

  • Size check: "Check this place" and Publish measure the place file as it would be sent and count its instanced meshes. A place file can be up to 8 MiB, and a place can hold 100,000 instanced meshes. Over a limit, the message gives the numbers, for example "The place file would be 9.4 MiB; Mantle accepts at most 8.0 MiB." or "This level has 106964 instanced meshes (foliage, PCG output, instanced components); a place holds at most 100000." When the place is over either limit, Publish stops with "This place is too big for Mantle (9.4 MiB, 106964 instanced meshes). Nothing was prepared or uploaded."
  • Thin and publish: when removing some foliage and instances would make the place fit, the Publish window shows a Thin and publish button, and the list below it says how many instances go. It removes foliage first and keeps some in every area, then other instances if it has to. Nothing is removed unless you press the button. Your level isn't changed, and the coverage report says how many were removed.
  • Identical meshes: 4 or more Static Mesh Actors with the same mesh and materials, and nothing else on them (no tags, no parent, no physics), publish as one instance set. The report says: "N actors that share a mesh and materials were published as M instance sets: each keeps its place, rotation and scale, but they are no longer separate named objects."
  • World Partition: Publish loads every cell first and lets them go when it ends. The Publish window shows a warning: "Loaded N World Partition actors in cells that were not loaded, so they publish with the rest (committed memory X MB, was Y MB)." It adds "They are unloaded again when the publish ends." If some actors still won't load, it adds "N actors are still not loaded (a data layer that is off, or a cell that would not load)." A big map uses a lot of memory while its cells are loaded: the message shows how much.
  • Level Instances: Publish also loads Level Instances that aren't loaded (nested ones too, up to 8 deep), then unloads the ones it loaded. The ones you loaded stay. The warning says "Loaded N Level Instance(s) that were not loaded in the editor, so what is inside them publishes with the rest." and, if some won't load, "N would not load, so what is inside them is left out."

Tools → Mantle → Check this place and Check what players receive don't load anything. On a World Partition level with unloaded cells, they stop with an error:

If you seeWhat to do
"This is a World Partition level, and N actors in cells the editor has not loaded (…) would be left out of the place.""Publish loads every cell itself. To check now, load the whole level: in the World Partition window select every cell and choose Load Region From Selection, or turn off Enable Streaming in World Settings."

To check the whole level without publishing, use Mantle.Check: see Checking from the console on the Creators overview page.

World text Live

A Text Render Actor publishes as text players read in the world, drawn in Mantle's own font. A Text Render component in a Blueprint prefab publishes as a part. Text that's empty, hidden in game or not visible is left out, and "Check what players receive" says so: "a text render actor with no text to read, or hidden in game, is not published."

  • Kept: the text (a line break or <br> starts a new line), color, world size (1 to 2,000 cm), horizontal alignment (left, center or right), vertical alignment (top, center or bottom) and the horizontal and vertical scale (0.1 to 10), with the actor's own scale folded in. A value outside its range is held to it, with a warning.
  • Not kept: the font, the text material, and letter and line spacing. Mantle draws them its own way, and "Check this place" warns: "has world text Mantle draws differently: …." The reasons it gives include "drawn in Mantle's font: its font '…' is not carried", "its letter and line spacing adjustments are not carried" and "its Quad Top alignment is published as the text's top". Its fix is "It publishes with the nearest look Mantle has."
  • Mirrored text: a negative scale on a Text Render Actor is an error, like on most actors: "has a scale of …; Mantle publishes scales above zero on every axis." Use a positive scale.
  • A place can have 256 texts, up to 512 characters each.
If you seeWhat to do
"has text N characters long; a place holds at most 512 per text."Shorten the text, or split it across several.
"is text number 257; a place holds at most 256."Remove some world text, or put several lines in one text.

Blueprint prefabs Live

You can place a Blueprint actor, such as a Fab prefab, as it is. Its static meshes (visible in game), point, spot and rect lights, Box and Sphere collision, audio components with a sound Mantle can publish, skeletal meshes that play one animation and particle systems each become a part of a group with the actor's name, transform and tags. Each part is named after its component, which is also the name scripts find it by.

  • Other parts a player could see or touch, such as capsules, decals, directional lights, hidden meshes that still collide and skeletal meshes that play more than one animation, are left out, and "Check this place" names each one with a warning, for example: "has a CapsuleComponent component ('…') that Mantle can't publish, so it is left out; the rest of the actor publishes." Components a player can't see or touch, such as a camera or spring arm, are named together: "has components Mantle drops when publishing: …. The rest of the actor publishes."
  • The Blueprint's graph logic isn't published, with a warning.
  • A prefab stays still unless it has Blueprint logic or you set one of its components to Movable yourself.
  • A Blueprint based on a Static Mesh Actor or Skeletal Mesh Actor publishes as a group of parts like any prefab, so a light added to it comes through. A Blueprint based on another class Mantle knows, such as Point Light, keeps that class's rules, so an extra mesh on it is still an error.

Sounds Live

An Ambient Sound publishes with your own Sound Wave, imported from a WAV.

  • Mono or stereo, 22,050 to 48,000 Hz, at most 60 seconds and 12 MiB. All the sounds a place uses can add up to 64 MiB once decoded.
  • MetaSounds can't be published: "is a MetaSound, and Mantle publishes sound waves only (a MetaSound is a graph players' clients don't run)". Edits made to the waveform inside Unreal aren't published either.
  • An Ambient Sound with a Sound Cue publishes as the one sound wave the cue plays first, with a warning: "uses Sound Cue '…'. Mantle publishes sound waves, so it publishes as the one wave the cue plays first, '…'; the cue's mixing and randomness are not kept." If the wave is the sound you want, you're done. Otherwise assign the sound wave you want to the Ambient Sound. A cue that plays no wave Mantle can publish is left out: see Content that's left out. A Sound Cue anywhere else, such as in Script Assets, is still an error: "is a Sound Cue, and Mantle publishes sound waves only (a cue is a graph players' clients don't run)".
  • An Ambient Sound with Auto Activate turned off waits for a script to play it.
  • Scripts can play any sound the place publishes, including sounds listed under Script Assets (script API 1.12).
  • Mantle has no built-in sounds.

Assets your scripts use Live

Everything placed in the level is published automatically. For a mesh, material or sound wave that only a script uses, add it to Script Assets under Mantle → Scripts in Place settings. It's checked and uploaded like placed content. The publish log has one line per asset, Script asset /Game/Props/SM_Boulder.SM_Boulder = ast_u…, and the read-only Script Asset Ids list shows the same ids until you close the level. Use the id in your script. A texture, Sound Cue, MetaSound or deleted asset in the list is an error. The script members that use these ids arrive in script API 1.8 and 1.12 (see Core concepts).

Decals Live

Give a Decal Actor a decal material (its Material Domain is Deferred Decal) with a texture parameter named BaseColor; its alpha is the decal's shape. Add a texture parameter named Normal for a normal map and a scalar parameter named Opacity (0 to 1) to fade it. Only these are published, not the rest of the material. Sort Order runs from -1,000 to 1,000, and Fade Screen Size from 0 (never fades) to 1.

A decal Mantle has no picture for is left out with a warning, and the rest of the place still publishes. Each message ends with "It is left out of the place." and "The rest of the place publishes without this decal."

If you seeWhat to do
"…which Mantle can't publish: a published decal is a texture, and this material sets no BaseColor texture."Add a texture parameter named BaseColor to the decal material.
"…which is not a decal material."This is a surface material (its Material Domain isn't Deferred Decal). Use a decal material.
"has no decal material."Give the Decal Actor a decal material.
"has a scale of (0, 0, 0), so it covers nothing and is left out of the place."Give it a scale above zero on every axis to keep it.
"has a scale of (…); Mantle places a decal at the positive scale, so its picture is not mirrored."Fine if the mirroring does not matter. Otherwise mirror the decal's texture and use a positive scale. The decal still publishes.

The first three messages also say: "Give the decal a decal material (Material Domain: Deferred Decal) with a texture parameter named BaseColor: its alpha is the decal's shape. Add a texture parameter named Normal for a normal map and a scalar parameter named Opacity to fade it." "Check this place" also gives a warning for each decal, as a reminder that the rest of the material is dropped: "Fine if the material only shows that texture. Otherwise, only the BaseColor and Normal textures and the Opacity value are kept: move anything else into them."

Animated meshes Live

A Skeletal Mesh Actor publishes with its own skeleton when its Animation Mode is Use Animation Asset with one animation. With no animation picked, it holds its reference pose. An Animation Blueprint is an error when it has an Anim Class set: "Set its Animation Mode to Use Animation Asset…". With no Anim Class, the mesh publishes holding its reference pose. Every player sees the clip in step, and scripts can change it (script API 1.18).

  • Mesh: at most 100,000 triangles, 8 material slots and 256 bones, with one LOD and no collision. A vertex weighted to more than 8 bones keeps its 8 heaviest, with a warning.
  • Clip: at most 60 seconds and 8 MiB once prepared for the game. Mantle samples it at 30 frames per second. A clip only plays on a mesh with the same skeleton.

Terrain Live

A Landscape publishes as a terrain. Each painted layer is drawn from the landscape's own material when Mantle can read it. To pick the look yourself, open Place settings and add an entry under Mantle → Terrain → Terrain Layers: the layer's name, the material to draw it with, and a tiling size in cm (400 by default). It is an error when Mantle can't read the landscape material at all and a layer has no entry ("has paint layer '…', which has no material to draw it with in Mantle."), or when no layer can be drawn ("has paint layers (…) that its landscape material does not use and Mantle Place Settings gives no entry, so players would see nothing on it."). A painted layer the material doesn't use is left out with a warning, and the landscape still publishes: "has painted layers its landscape material does not use (…), so they are not drawn and their paint is left out."

  • One landscape per place, with up to 8 painted layers. A slope overlay in the landscape material takes one of those, leaving 7. If a landscape has more, Mantle keeps the most painted and folds the others into the nearest by color, with a warning.
  • A landscape with nothing painted still publishes. It is drawn with its landscape material's first layer, or plain grey, with a warning: "has no painted layer and no material in Mantle Place Settings to draw it with, so it is drawn with the first look its landscape material states ('…')." (or "…so it is drawn with plain grey."). To draw the ground differently, add an entry under Mantle → Terrain → Terrain Layers. It is only an error when even that can't be made: "has no painted layer and no material to draw it with, so players would see nothing on it."
  • Landscape holes are filled, not refused. The published terrain has no holes, so the ground is drawn across them, with a warning: "has holes painted in it (its visibility layer, …), which Mantle terrain cannot carry yet, so the published terrain is filled across them." Your landscape isn't changed. To keep an opening, such as a cave mouth or a tunnel, model it as a mesh over the filled ground.
  • A landscape material that blends layers by slope and height is read too, including snow or water edges that use a noise texture. Each layer also keeps its own roughness. Anything Mantle can't keep is a warning, for example "edge noise from the texture '…' in a mask is taken as the texture's average: the noise is not kept."
  • The samples must be 1 to 10,000 cm apart. A landscape more than 2,049 samples on a side is resampled, with a warning.
  • Grass: the grass types on the landscape material publish as grass that every player's game grows near the camera, up to 32 grass entries and 4 grass maps, with a density up to 100 instances per square meter, drawn out to at most 50,000 cm. Anything past that is clamped or left out, with a warning.
  • Landscape splines themselves aren't published, but the spline meshes on them (roads, say) are baked into static meshes like any other spline meshes.

Foliage and instanced meshes Live

Painted foliage, Instanced Static Mesh components and generated PCG output publish as instanced meshes: at most 100,000 instances in a place. Spline meshes are baked into ordinary static meshes, merged by material, and cut into pieces that fit the mesh limits. PCG set to Generate At Runtime or not generated yet and unsaved dynamic meshes are errors. Per-instance custom data is dropped with a warning.

  • Mirrored instances: an instance with a negative scale publishes as an instance of a mirrored copy of the mesh, at the positive scale. "Check this place" warns: "has N mirrored instance(s) of … (a negative scale on one axis); they publish as a mirrored copy of the mesh." Its fix is "Nothing to do. A mesh that can't be read for the copy is left out, and the coverage report says so."
  • Zero-scale instances: they're left out, with a warning: "has N instance(s) of … scaled to zero; they are left out." Its fix is "Nothing to do: an instance with no size can't be seen or touched."

Far-off meshes and instances stop being drawn, so a big map stays smooth. A mesh's own Desired Max Draw Distance (or Instance End Cull Distance for foliage and instanced meshes) is used when it has one. Otherwise Mantle picks a distance from the mesh's size. Big meshes are always drawn. See Meshes for the numbers.

Physics objects Live

Tick Simulate Physics on a Static Mesh Actor to make it a physics object: the server simulates it and every player sees it move. Its mass (0.1 to 100,000 kg), damping and gravity are kept. Give it the tag MantleAnchored to start it pinned in place until a script lets it go. It simulates on the mesh's simple collision, or a box the size of the mesh when it has none, with a warning. Scripts can push it and listen for hits (script API 1.15).

Its surface comes from its Phys Material Override, else its mesh's simple-collision physical material. Mantle maps it to one of six presets: default, rubber, ice, metal, wood or stone, each with its own friction, bounciness and density. Mantle's own preset materials map exactly; anything else maps by name (rubber/bouncy/bounce, ice/icy/slippery, metal/metallic/steel/iron/aluminum/aluminium, wood/wooden/timber/plank, stone/rock/concrete/brick/marble) or by how closely its friction and bounciness match a preset. If nothing matches, it publishes as default and "Check this place" names the actor and the material. Scripts can read and change the surface with PhysicsMaterial (script API 1.28).

Players push a physics object gently as they walk into it: a light object slides rather than flying off, and standing on one doesn't spin it or pop you off. A physics object heavy enough that a player's push can't slide it can be climbed or vaulted like static geometry; a lighter one can't, so a light crate that would tip or slide away is jumped over, not onto. To make something climbable, make it heavier, anchor it, or use static (non-physics) geometry instead.

Surfaces Live

Every mesh and terrain layer is made of something, and Mantle calls that its surface. The surface picks what players see where something touches it: the bullet impact and the bullet hole it leaves, the dust or spray a tire throws, and the sound of a footstep (with a puff of dust on loose ground). The surfaces are default, concrete, stone, brick, asphalt, wood, metal, glass, dirt, grass, sand, mud, snow, ice, water, rubber, plastic, fabric, flesh, gravel and leaf. Anything you don't mark is default.

Mantle picks the surface from the name of a physical material. Name yours after the surface, in any case and with or without PM_ in front: Brick, PM_Brick and brick all mean brick. The game's own materials (PM_MantleSurface_Brick, PM_MantleMetal and the rest) match too, but the plugin doesn't include them.

  • A mesh: set Collision → Phys Material Override to a physical material named after the surface. Without an override, the physical material of the mesh's first material counts, so setting Phys Material on a material instance marks every mesh that uses it. Instanced meshes and foliage are marked the same way.
  • A landscape: set Phys Material on each paint layer's layer info. A hit on the terrain uses the surface of the layer painted most at that spot.
  • A physics object already takes its surface from its preset, so there's nothing extra to mark.
  • Only the whole name counts. Brick marks brick, but BrickWall or a material named anything else publishes as default: Mantle doesn't guess from similar words or values here. Rename it, or use a new physical material with a matching name.
  • A surface is mostly about looks. The ones that are also physics presets (ice, rubber, metal, wood, stone) bring their friction and bounciness with them, so an ice floor is slippery.

Particle effects Live

Place a Mantle Effect (Place Actors → All Classes) and pick one of five built-in effects: Smoke, Fire, Sparks, Dust or Glow. You can change its color, particle size (1 to 1,000 cm), rate (up to 200 a second), speed (up to 5,000 cm/s) and lifetime (0.05 to 10 s). Autoplay starts it when a player joins; with it off, the effect waits for a script. Loop keeps it emitting; with it off, it emits for one second each time it starts. Particles leave along the actor's up direction. A place can have 64 effects.

The Sprites effect is a general emitter you tune yourself, and the only one that takes your own image. Scripts can't pick it. It has:

  • Look: a texture (empty draws a soft round dot), Blend (Translucent or Additive), color at the start and end of a particle's life, opacity at its peak (0 to 1) and at its end and a fade-in (0 to 1 of its life).
  • Size and life: a size range (0.1 to 1,000 cm), the size at the end as a multiple of the start (0 to 10) and a lifetime range (0.05 to 10 s).
  • Movement: a velocity range and an acceleration, each −5,000 to 5,000 cm/s (cm/s² for acceleration) on every axis. Gravity is negative Z.
  • Where it starts: Point, Box, Sphere or Cylinder, with its size, and up to 2,000 particles at once.
  • Flipbook: up to 16 columns and 16 rows of frames in the texture (256 frames). Random picks one per particle; Over Life plays them in order.

You can't upload your own particle systems. A Cascade particle system in your level is converted to Sprites effects as closely as it can be, and Check what players receive lists what changed. A Niagara system is left out.

Crowds Live

A Mantle Crowd Spawner fills lanes you draw with ambient people who walk them. Each spawner has 1 to 100 people, 1 to 16 lanes of 2 to 64 points, up to 16 looks, and up to 16 points of interest that people step off their lane to visit. A place can have 8 spawners and 200 people in all. Scripts can thin or pause a crowd (script API 1.16) and reseed it (script API 1.21).

Scatter fields Live

Place a Mantle Scatter Volume (Place Actors, search "Mantle Scatter") to fill a box with a field of instanced meshes, such as rocks or grass clumps, without painting foliage by hand. Set Half Extent (half the box's size on each axis, in cm; Z 0 is a flat layer) and Density (instances per square meter of the box's footprint), add 1 to 16 Meshes (each with a Weight and optional material overrides), pick a scale range and an Alignment (Upright turns instances about their vertical axis only, Tilted also leans them up to Max Tilt Degrees, Tumbled turns them any way), and set a Seed. The level shows the field as you edit it, and moving the volume moves the field without reshuffling it; changing the seed does.

  • The seed and settings decide the field, so it looks the same for everyone, and even a large field doesn't slow the server down.
  • Instances are visual only: no collision, and nothing for navigation. Walking through a scatter field never blocks you.
  • At most 200,000 instances from one Scatter volume, and 200,000 in a place in all. "Check this place" names a volume with no meshes, or set to make more instances than the cap allows.

Joints Live

Place an engine Physics Constraint Actor between two Static Mesh Actors that have Simulate Physics on (its first constrained actor is the moving one; leave the second empty to join it to the world instead of another object) to publish a joint. It becomes one of three kinds, depending on how you set it up:

KindSet the Constraint Actor's Linear and Angular limits to
WeldLinear X, Y and Z Locked, and Swing 1, Swing 2 and Twist Locked. The two move as one.
HingeLinear X, Y and Z Locked, and exactly one of Twist, Swing 1 or Swing 2 set to Free or Limited (its Angular Rotation Offset biases the limit), the other two Locked. The starting angle must be inside the limit.
RopeLinear X, Y and Z all Limited (Linear Limit Size is the rope's length), and every angle Free.

Anything else, such as a motor or drive, a partially locked linear axis, two free angles, or an end that isn't set to simulate, is left out of the place, and "Check this place" names it with the fix. Tick Linear Breakable or Angular Breakable and set its Break Threshold to give the joint a break force, so a hard enough hit snaps a weld or hinge, or lets a rope tear free (a rope reads only the linear one); leave both unticked for a joint that never breaks. A place can have 200 joints; "Check this place" warns above 100. Every player sees the two objects move as the joint holds them, and scripts can create, listen to and remove joints too (see Constraints in the API reference).

Reflection captures Live

A Box Reflection Capture or Sphere Reflection Capture publishes as a reflection probe. Nothing baked is kept: every player's game captures the probe again, at run time, in the place's own lighting. So a baked capture is replaced, not kept.

  • A box's size is the capture's scale (a half extent of 1 to 100,000 cm) with a transition distance of 1 to 1,000 cm. A sphere has an influence radius of 8 to 16,384 cm. Brightness runs 0 to 8, and the sky light's leak 0 to 1. A value outside its range is clamped.
  • A hidden capture is left out, and "Check what players receive" lists it. Every capture past the 64 a place can have is left out with a warning.

Weapon spawns Live

A Mantle Weapon Spawn leaves a weapon where players can pick it up. Build the weapon first with Tools → Mantle → Build a weapon... ("Turn a 3D model of a gun into a weapon players can use."), then add the spawn with Tools → Mantle → Add Weapon Spawn and set:

  • Weapon: a weapon you built. The list shows each weapon in your project's MantleWeapons folder that has its files. Publishing sends it with your place.
  • Storage Item: a weapon from your place's storage instead, by its Name in Place settings → Storage. Set a Weapon or a Storage Item, not both.
  • Respawn Seconds: how long after a player picks it up until a new one appears (0 to 600, 10 by default).
  • Enabled: untick it to keep the spawn in your place without a weapon. A script can still use it.

A place can have 32 weapon spawns. "Check this place" names a spawn with no weapon, one that sets both and a weapon whose files aren't in your MantleWeapons folder.

Assets

Built-in library Live

The library has 6 items. Use them straight from this folder so Mantle recognizes them:

  • Meshes: the cube, sphere, cylinder, cone and plane from /Engine/BasicShapes.
  • Materials: one basic material, BasicShapeMaterial, from /Engine/BasicShapes.
  • The library has no sounds or textures. Anything else, such as the meshes and materials in /Game/LevelPrototyping, counts as your own and is uploaded when you publish: see Your own assets.

Your own assets Live

You can use static meshes, materials and sounds from your own project, such as a Fab pack. You don't upload them yourself: when you publish, Mantle picks up every mesh, material and sound the level uses that isn't in the library and gets it ready for the game. Later publishes are quicker, because files you've already uploaded aren't sent again. For the full walkthrough, every limit and message, and how to fix problems, see Custom meshes and materials.

Your own vehicles Live

Tools → Mantle → Build a vehicle... makes a vehicle from a kind you pick, and Tools → Mantle → Convert vehicle turns a car's skeletal mesh into a Mantle vehicle. A Mantle Vehicle Spawn places it in your level. See the Vehicles guide.

Sky and environment

  • Live Default sky: Mantle adds an atmosphere, a real-time sky light, clouds and fog to your world. If your place has no Directional Light, Mantle also adds a sun (intensity 3.5).
  • Live Day, night and weather: every server shares a day that lasts 30 real minutes and starts at 9:00, with sunrise at 6:00, sunset at 18:00, and a moon with phases and stars at night. The weather changes on its own between clear (32%), partly cloudy (25%), cloudy (15%), overcast (10%), light rain (6%), rain (5%), foggy (5%) and storms with lightning (2%); after a storm comes rain, light rain or overcast. Each weather lasts 12 to 20 minutes and eases in over 90 seconds. Light snow, snow, blizzard, dust calm and dust storm never come on their own: a place or a script has to ask for them. A level with a sun or sky of its own doesn't change on its own: see Your level's own look. Scripts can read and change the time and weather (script API 1.4 and 1.11). Rain, snow and dust fall around the camera and stop under cover. Rain, wind and thunder have sound, quieter indoors.
  • Live Lights are all Movable in the published world, and no lighting is baked.
  • Planned Custom sky.
  • Live Drawn water: a mesh with a Single Layer Water material shows as water, with no waves and no swimming. See Water.
  • Next release Water bodies: the Water plugin's Ocean, Lake, River and Custom bodies. The game can't read them yet, so don't publish a level with them. See Water.

Your level's own look Live

Mantle supplies its own sky, but the editor reads some of the look from the actors in your level. "Check what players receive" lists what it kept and what it left out.

  • The sun: the first Directional Light keeps its intensity and color (a temperature is folded into the color). Its angle comes from the clock: if you set a Clock Time, or a Time Scale other than 1, in Place settings, they decide. If you didn't, and the light is at least 1° above the horizon, its angle becomes the place's clock, stopped at that hour with Time Scale 0, so the day and night cycle is off. Set Clock Time in Place settings to run the day from an hour you choose. A light less than 1° above the horizon leaves the clock alone, and so does a light that's attached to another actor. The clock's sun peaks at 60°, so a higher light is set to that. Its heading (yaw) is kept roughly: it is turned to fit the clock's sun.
  • Weather in a level with its own look: a level with a sun, sky atmosphere, sky light or volumetric cloud has one look, so the place holds its weather instead of letting it change. It holds Clear, Partly Cloudy or Overcast from the first cloud's coverage, or Clear when there are no clouds. Start a new place adds a sun, so a new place holds Clear until you choose another.
  • Choosing the weather: pick a Weather in Place settings for a different one. Natural lets the weather change on its own, even in a level with a sun or sky of its own. The default, Level's Sky, holds the weather as above in such a level, and lets it change on its own in a level without one.
  • What the report says: "Check what players receive" tells you which it did. With Natural chosen:"Mantle Place Settings chooses Natural, so the place holds no weather and it changes on its own, though the level has a sky of its own." With no clouds and the default: "the level has no volumetric cloud, so the place's weather is held at Clear instead of changing on its own; choose Natural or another Weather in Mantle Place Settings for a different one."
  • Post Process Volume: its saturation, bloom, white balance (as a color tint) and exposure are kept, from one volume (the enabled one with the highest priority). The rest of its settings aren't. Saturation, Bloom and Color Tint set in Place settings win over the volume's. A level with a sun, sky, fog or post-process volume also keeps its exposure, even without a volume, unless that exposure matches the game's own.
  • Exponential Height Fog: the first fog's density, height falloff, inscattering color, start distance and directional inscattering color are kept, if you've changed them from Unreal's default fog. They're held to the limits the place allows. The fog sits at ground level, whatever its height in the level.
  • Sky Atmosphere: the place has no setting for it, so Mantle's own is used. Volumetric clouds publish only their coverage, as the weather.
  • Sky dome: a level's own sky dome mesh, such as BP_Sky_Sphere, is left out, because Mantle supplies the sky. "Check this place" warns: "a sky mesh; Mantle supplies its own sky, so it isn't published." Inside a Blueprint, it says "has a sky mesh ('…'); Mantle supplies its own sky, so it isn't published." The rest of the actor publishes as usual.
  • Sky Light: Mantle uses its own sky light, but at the strength of your level's Sky Light, so an indoor level isn't lit by a sky it never shows. A Sky Light that uses a cubemap is read too, including an HDR panorama. To set it yourself, tick Sky Light Intensity in Place settings (0 is none, 1 is full). If Mantle can't read the brightness, it uses full strength and says why in "Check what players receive".
  • The sun indoors: Direct Sun in Place settings is Automatic by default: if the sun reaches none of your Player Starts, as in a sealed room, only the sky lights the place. Choose Always to keep the sun or Never to turn it off. The report says "no direct sun: the sun reaches none of the level's N spawn point(s), as in the editor's baked lighting, so only the sky lights the place; set Direct Sun to Always in Mantle Place Settings to keep it."

The place's default look Live

Without touching a script, you can set the look every player starts in: choose Tools → Mantle → Place settings and open the Mantle | Environment category. Every field is optional. A field you leave alone isn't published, though a sun or sky in your level can still set the clock, the weather, the sky light's strength and whether the sun shines in: see Your level's own look.

FieldWhat it sets

Natural lets it change on its own in any level. A server script's changes to the environment still win over these: they're the starting point a script's writes build on, not a floor under them. See Environment in the API reference for the full script API.

Water In progress

Water that's drawn works today. Water bodies from the Water plugin are exported, but the game doesn't build them yet.

  • Live Drawn water: a mesh whose material uses Single Layer Water publishes as water. So does a water material that fades with depth, which many Fab rivers and lakes use, when its deep water is nearly solid (90% or more). The surface, roughness and normal maps are kept, and so is how the water darkens with depth. It's a surface you see: there are no waves and no swimming, and nothing floats on it. "Check what players receive" says what it kept.
  • Next release Water bodies: the Water plugin's Ocean, Lake, River and Custom bodies, and the level's Water Zone, export as water bodies with their shape (the spline), depth, waves (a river has none), collision and look colors. A landscape that a body carves keeps the carve. Don't publish a level with Water plugin bodies yet. The game can't read them yet, so players on the current game may not be able to load the place at all. Remove the bodies, or keep them in a level you don't publish, until the release that supports them. Swimming and floating aren't available.
  • The surface mesh the Water plugin makes for a body (a lake's surface, say) isn't published as a mesh of its own.
  • A place can have 64 water bodies, each with up to 256 spline points and up to 64 waves, and 4,096 spline points in all. A river needs at least 2 spline points, and a lake or an ocean needs 3. A body with more waves keeps the highest ones.

"Check what players receive" reports each body. A body that can't publish is left out of the place, and the rest publishes:
