Actor names and tags are kept, and actors set to Movable stay dynamic. There's no visibility setting, so hidden actors show up in the published world.

If something is standing on a Player Start when a player spawns, for example a parked car or a physics prop, the player is placed on the nearest clear spot with ground under it, up to 8 m away. If nothing within 8 m is clear, the player is placed on the start anyway. Keep your Player Starts clear of Vehicle Spawns and anything else that moves.

What happens when you publish

  • Left out with a warning: BSP brushes, engine volumes (such as Trigger Volume and Blocking Volume), placed pawns, cameras ("Mantle runs every player's camera itself") and Text Render Actors. Use static meshes instead, and use Trigger Box or Trigger Sphere actors for trigger areas.
  • Kept in part, with a warning: SkyAtmosphere, SkyLight, VolumetricCloud and ExponentialHeightFog ("Mantle provides the sky; this actor's settings are not published."), and Post Process Volumes. Mantle keeps some of their look and supplies the rest. See Your level's own look.
  • Left out with a warning: Blueprint logic, in actors and in the Level Blueprint: "Blueprint logic isn't published: the place gets this actor's components, not its behaviour." Use scripts for behaviour.
  • Converted and uploaded: meshes, materials and sounds from your project, and your Script Assets. See Your own assets.
  • Uploaded with the place: the scripts in the level's Scripts folder, and the UI documents (.rml), stylesheets (.rcss) and PNG or JPEG images in its UI folder, next to Scripts. Other files in UI are ignored. See UI documents.
  • Stops the publish with an error: unsupported actors, extra components on an actor Mantle knows by class (such as a Static Mesh Actor with a light added), names, tags or material overrides over the limits below, a zero or negative scale, meshes over the mesh limits, UI files that break the UI document limits, an empty level, and a World Partition level with cells the editor hasn't loaded (load the whole level first). Blueprint prefabs Live

You can place a Blueprint actor, such as a Fab prefab, as it is. Its static meshes (visible in game), point and spot lights, Box and Sphere collision, and audio components with a sound Mantle can publish each become a part of a group with the actor's name, transform and tags. Each part is named after its component, which is also the name scripts find it by.

  • Other parts a player could see or touch, such as capsules, skeletal meshes, decals, directional lights and hidden meshes that still collide, are left out, and "Check this place" names them: "has components Mantle drops when publishing: …. The rest of the actor publishes."
  • The Blueprint's graph logic isn't published, with a warning.
  • A prefab stays still unless it has Blueprint logic or you set one of its components to Movable yourself.
  • A Blueprint based on a class Mantle already knows, such as Static Mesh Actor, keeps that class's rules, so an extra light on it is still an error.

Sounds Live

An Ambient Sound publishes with your own Sound Wave, imported from a WAV.

  • Mono or stereo, 22,050 to 48,000 Hz, at most 60 seconds and 12 MiB. All the sounds a place uses can add up to 64 MiB once decoded.
  • Sound Cues and MetaSounds can't be published: "is a Sound Cue, and Mantle publishes sound waves only (a cue is a graph players' clients don't run)". Edits made to the waveform inside Unreal aren't published either.
  • An Ambient Sound with Auto Activate turned off waits for a script to play it.
  • Scripts can play any sound the place publishes, including sounds listed under Script Assets (script API 1.12).
  • Mantle has no built-in sounds.

Assets your scripts use Live

Everything placed in the level is published automatically. For a mesh, material or sound wave that only a script uses, add it to Script Assets under Mantle → Scripts in Place settings. It's checked and uploaded like placed content. The publish log has one line per asset, Script asset /Game/Props/SM_Boulder.SM_Boulder = ast_u…, and the read-only Script Asset Ids list shows the same ids until you close the editor. Use the id in your script. A texture, Sound Cue, MetaSound or deleted asset in the list is an error. The script members that use these ids arrive in script API 1.8 and 1.12 (see Core concepts).

Decals Live

Give a Decal Actor a Material Instance of M_MantleDecal, or M_MantleDecal_Normal for a decal with a normal map. The base colour's alpha is the decal's shape. With your own decal material, only its base colour (and normal) texture is published, with a warning. A surface material is refused, and the message names M_MantleDecal. Sort Order runs from -1,000 to 1,000, and Fade Screen Size from 0 (never fades) to 1.

Animated meshes Live

A Skeletal Mesh Actor publishes with its own skeleton when its Animation Mode is Use Animation Asset with one animation. An Animation Blueprint is an error: "Set its Animation Mode to Use Animation Asset…". Every player sees the clip in step, and scripts can change it (script API 1.18).

  • Mesh: at most 100,000 triangles, 8 material slots, 256 bones and 8 bone influences per vertex, one LOD, no collision.
  • Clip: at most 60 seconds, 60 frames per second, 256 bones and 8 MiB. A clip only plays on a mesh with the same skeleton.

Terrain Live

A Landscape publishes as a terrain. Each painted layer is drawn from the landscape's own material when Mantle can read it. To pick the look yourself, open Place settings and add an entry under Mantle → Terrain → Terrain Layers: the layer's name, the material to draw it with, and a tiling size in cm (400 by default). It is an error only when Mantle can't read the landscape material at all and a layer has no entry ("has paint layer '…', which has no material to draw it with in Mantle."), or when no layer can be drawn ("has paint layers (…) that its landscape material does not use and Mantle Place Settings gives no entry, so players would see nothing on it."). A painted layer the material doesn't use is left out with a warning, and the landscape still publishes: "has painted layers its landscape material does not use (…), so they are not drawn and their paint is left out."

  • One landscape per place, with up to 8 painted layers. If a landscape has more, Mantle keeps the most painted and folds the others into the nearest by colour, with a warning.
  • Landscape holes are refused: "has holes painted in it (its visibility layer), which Mantle cannot publish yet." Erase them with the Visibility tool or cover them with meshes.
  • The samples must be 1 to 10,000 cm apart. A landscape more than 2,049 samples on a side is resampled, with a warning.
  • Grass: the grass types on the landscape material publish as grass that every player's game grows near the camera, up to 32 grass entries and 4 grass maps, with a density up to 100 instances per square metre, drawn out to at most 50,000 cm. Anything past that is clamped or left out, with a warning.
  • Landscape splines aren't published.

Foliage and instanced meshes Live

Painted foliage, Instanced Static Mesh components and generated PCG output publish as instanced meshes: at most 100,000 instances in a place. Spline meshes are baked into ordinary static meshes, merged by material, and have to fit the mesh limits. Mirrored or zero-scale instances, PCG set to Generate At Runtime or not generated yet and unsaved dynamic meshes are errors. Per-instance custom data is dropped with a warning.

Physics objects Live

Tick Simulate Physics on a Static Mesh Actor to make it a physics object: the server simulates it and every player sees it move. Its mass (0.1 to 100,000 kg), damping and gravity are kept. Give it the tag MantleAnchored to start it pinned in place until a script lets it go. It simulates on the mesh's simple collision, or a box the size of the mesh when it has none, with a warning. Scripts can push it and listen for hits (script API 1.15).

Its surface comes from its Phys Material Override, else its mesh's simple-collision physical material. Mantle maps it to one of six presets: default, rubber, ice, metal, wood or stone, each with its own friction, bounciness and density. Mantle's own preset materials map exactly; anything else maps by name (rubber/bouncy, ice/icy/slippery, metal/steel/iron/aluminum, wood/wooden/timber/plank, stone/rock/concrete/brick/marble) or by how closely its friction and bounciness match a preset. If nothing matches, it publishes as default and "Check this place" names the actor and the material. Scripts can read and change it (script API 1.28).

Players push a physics object gently as they walk into it: a light object slides rather than flying off, and standing on one doesn't spin it or pop you off. A physics object heavy enough that a player's push can't slide it can be climbed or vaulted like static geometry; a lighter one can't, so a light crate that would tip or slide away is jumped over, not onto. To make something climbable, make it heavier, anchor it, or use static (non-physics) geometry instead.

Surfaces Live

Every mesh and terrain layer is made of something, and Mantle calls that its surface. The surface picks what players see where something touches it: the bullet impact and the bullet hole it leaves, the dust or spray a tire throws, and the sound of a footstep (with a puff of dust on loose ground). The surfaces are default, concrete, stone, brick, asphalt, wood, metal, glass, dirt, grass, sand, mud, snow, ice, water, rubber, plastic, fabric, flesh, gravel and leaf. Anything you don't mark is default.

Mantle ships one physical material per surface: the physics presets in MantleCore → Physics (PM_MantleMetal and the rest) and the others in MantleCore → Physics → Surfaces (PM_MantleSurface_Brick, PM_MantleSurface_Grass and so on).

  • A mesh: set Collision → Phys Material Override to one of them. Without an override, the physical material of the mesh's first material counts, so setting Phys Material on a material instance marks every mesh that uses it. Instanced meshes and foliage are marked the same way.
  • A landscape: set Phys Material on each paint layer's layer info. A hit on the terrain uses the surface of the layer painted most at that spot.
  • A physics object already takes its surface from its preset, so there's nothing extra to mark.
  • Only Mantle's own physical materials count. One from another pack, even one called "Brick", publishes as default; swap it for Mantle's.
  • A surface is mostly about looks. The ones that are also physics presets (ice, rubber, metal, wood, stone) bring their friction and bounciness with them, so an ice floor is slippery.

Particle effects Live

Place a Mantle Effect (Place Actors → All Classes) and pick one of five built-in effects: Smoke, Fire, Sparks, Dust or Glow. You can change its colour, particle size (1 to 1,000 cm), rate (up to 200 a second), speed (up to 5,000 cm/s) and lifetime (0.05 to 10 s). Autoplay starts it when a player joins; with it off, the effect waits for a script. Loop keeps it emitting; with it off, it emits for one second each time it starts. Particles leave along the actor's up direction. A place can have 64 effects.

The Sprites effect is a general emitter you tune yourself, and the only one that takes your own image. Scripts can't pick it. It has:

  • Look: a texture (empty draws a soft round dot), Blend (Translucent or Additive), colour at the start and end of a particle's life, opacity at its peak (0 to 1) and at its end and a fade-in (0 to 1 of its life).
  • Size and life: a size range (0.1 to 1,000 cm), the size at the end as a multiple of the start (0 to 10) and a lifetime range (0.05 to 10 s).
  • Movement: a velocity range and an acceleration, each −5,000 to 5,000 cm/s (cm/s² for acceleration) on every axis. Gravity is negative Z.
  • Where it starts: Point, Box, Sphere or Cylinder, with its size, and up to 2,000 particles at once.
  • Flipbook: up to 16 columns and 16 rows of frames in the texture (256 frames). Random picks one per particle; Over Life plays them in order.

You can't upload your own particle systems.

Crowds Live

A Mantle Crowd Spawner fills lanes you draw with ambient people who walk them. Each spawner has 1 to 100 people, 1 to 16 lanes of 2 to 64 points, up to 16 looks, and up to 16 points of interest that people step off their lane to visit. A place can have 8 spawners and 200 people in all. Scripts can thin, pause or reseed a crowd (script API 1.16).

Scatter fields Live

Place a Mantle Scatter Volume (Place Actors, search "Mantle Scatter") to fill a box with a field of instanced meshes, such as rocks or grass clumps, without painting foliage by hand. Set Half Extent (the box's size, in cm; Z 0 is a flat layer) and Density (instances per square metre of the box's footprint), add 1 to 16 Meshes (each with a Weight and optional material overrides), pick a scale range and an Alignment (Upright turns instances about their vertical axis only, Tilted also leans them up to Max Tilt, Tumbled turns them any way), and set a Seed. The level shows the field as you edit it, and moving the volume moves the field without reshuffling it; changing the seed does.

  • The seed and settings decide the field, so it looks the same for everyone, and even a large field doesn't slow the server down.
  • Instances are visual only: no collision, and nothing for navigation. Walking through a scatter field never blocks you.
  • At most 200,000 instances from one Scatter volume, and 200,000 in a place in all. "Check this place" names a volume with no meshes, or set to make more instances than the cap allows.

Joints Live

Place an engine Physics Constraint Actor between two Static Mesh Actors that have Simulate Physics on (its first constrained actor is the moving one; leave the second empty to join it to the world instead of another object) to publish a joint. It becomes one of three kinds, depending on how you set it up:

KindSet the Constraint Actor's Linear and Angular limits to
WeldLinear X, Y and Z Locked, and Swing 1, Swing 2 and Twist Locked. The two move as one.
HingeLinear X, Y and Z Locked, and exactly one of Twist, Swing 1 or Swing 2 set to Free or Limited (its Angular Rotation Offset biases the limit), the other two Locked. The starting angle must be inside the limit.
RopeLinear X, Y and Z all Limited (Linear Limit Size is the rope's length), and every angle Free.

Anything else, such as a motor or drive, a partially locked linear axis, two free angles, or an end that isn't set to simulate, is left out of the place, and "Check this place" names it with the fix. Linear and Angular Break Threshold become the joint's break force, so a hard enough hit snaps a weld or hinge, or lets a rope tear free; leave them at 0 for a joint that never breaks. A place can have 200 joints; "Check this place" warns above 100. Every player sees the two objects move as the joint holds them, and scripts can create, listen to and remove joints too (see the scripting guide).

Reflection captures Live

A Box Reflection Capture or Sphere Reflection Capture publishes as a reflection probe. Nothing baked is kept: every player's game captures the probe again, at run time, in the place's own lighting. So a baked capture is replaced, not kept.

  • A box's size is the capture's scale (a half extent of 1 to 100,000 cm) with a transition distance of 1 to 1,000 cm. A sphere has an influence radius of 8 to 16,384 cm. Brightness runs 0 to 8, and the sky light's leak 0 to 1. A value outside its range is clamped.
  • A Plane Reflection Capture has no place form and is left out.
  • A hidden capture, and every capture past the 64 a place can have, is left out with a warning.

Weapon spawns Live

A Mantle Weapon Spawn leaves a weapon where players can pick it up. Build the weapon first with Tools → Mantle → Build a weapon ("Turn a 3D model of a gun into a weapon players can use."), then place the spawn and set:

  • Weapon: a weapon you built. The list shows every weapon in your project's MantleWeapons folder. Publishing sends it with your place.
  • Storage Item: a weapon from your place's storage instead, by its Name in Place settings → Storage. Set a Weapon or a Storage Item, not both.
  • Respawn Seconds: how long after a player picks it up until a new one appears (0 to 600, 10 by default).
  • Enabled: untick it to keep the spawn in your place without a weapon. A script can still use it.

A place can have 32 weapon spawns. "Check this place" names a spawn with no weapon, one that sets both and a weapon whose files aren't in your MantleWeapons folder.

Assets

Built-in library Live

The library has 15 items. Use them straight from these folders so Mantle recognises them:

  • Meshes: the cube, sphere, cylinder, cone and plane from /Engine/BasicShapes, and the cube, plane, ramp, quarter cylinder, chamfered cube and cylinder from /Game/LevelPrototyping.
  • Materials: a basic material and three prototype grid materials.
  • The library has no sounds or textures. The prototyping meshes don't all have their pivot in the same place.

Your own assets Live

You can use static meshes, materials and sounds from your own project, such as a Fab pack. You don't upload them yourself: when you publish, Mantle picks up every mesh, material and sound the level uses that isn't in the library and gets it ready for the game. Later publishes are quicker, because files you've already uploaded aren't sent again. For the full walkthrough, every limit and message, and how to fix problems, see Custom meshes and materials.

Your own vehicles Live

Tools → Mantle → Build a vehicle makes a vehicle from a kind you pick, and Tools → Mantle → Convert vehicle turns a car's skeletal mesh into a Mantle vehicle. A Mantle Vehicle Spawn places it in your level. See the Vehicles guide.

Sky and environment

  • Live Default sky: Mantle adds an atmosphere, a real-time sky light, clouds and fog to your world. If your place has no Directional Light, Mantle also adds a sun (intensity 3.5).
  • Live Day, night and weather: every server shares a day that lasts 30 real minutes and starts at 9:00, with sunrise at 6:00, sunset at 18:00, and a moon with phases and stars at night. The weather changes on its own between clear (32%), partly cloudy (25%), cloudy (15%), overcast (10%), light rain (6%), rain (5%), foggy (5%) and storms with lightning (2%); after a storm comes rain, light rain or overcast. Light snow, snow, blizzard, dust calm and dust storm never come on their own: a place or a script has to ask for them. A level with a sun or sky of its own doesn't change on its own: see Your level's own look. Scripts can read and change the time and weather (script API 1.4 and 1.11). Rain, snow and dust fall around the camera and stop under cover. Rain, wind and thunder have sound, quieter indoors.
  • Live Lights are all Movable in the published world, and no lighting is baked.
  • Planned Custom sky.
  • Planned Water support.

Your level's own look Live

Mantle supplies its own sky, but the editor reads some of the look from the actors in your level. "Check what players receive" lists what it kept and what it left out.

  • The sun: the first Directional Light keeps its intensity and colour (a temperature is folded into the colour). Its angle comes from the clock: if you set Clock Time or Time Scale in Place settings, they decide. If you didn't, and the light is at least 1° above the horizon, its angle becomes the place's clock, stopped at that hour with Time Scale 0, so the day and night cycle is off. Set Clock Time in Place settings to run the day from an hour you choose. A light at or below the horizon leaves the clock alone. Its heading (yaw) is kept roughly: it is turned to fit the clock's sun.
  • Weather: a level with a sun, sky atmosphere, sky light or volumetric cloud of its own has one look, so the place holds its weather rather than letting it change on its own: Clear, Partly Cloudy or Overcast from the first cloud's coverage, or Clear when there are no clouds. Choose a Weather in Place settings for a different one. Natural writes no weather, so it doesn't bring the changing weather back: only a level without a sun or sky of its own gets weather that changes on its own. Start a new place adds a sun, so a new place holds Clear until you choose another.
  • Post Process Volume: its saturation, bloom, white balance (as a colour tint) and exposure are kept, from one volume (the enabled one with the highest priority). The rest of its settings aren't. Saturation and Bloom set in Place settings win over the volume's. A level with a sun, sky, fog or post-process volume also keeps its exposure, even without a volume.
  • Exponential Height Fog: the first fog's density, height falloff, inscattering colour, start distance and directional inscattering colour are kept, where they differ from Mantle's own fog. The fog sits at ground level, whatever its height in the level.
  • Sky Atmosphere and Sky Light: the place has no setting for them, so Mantle's own are used. Volumetric clouds publish only their coverage, as the weather.

The place's default look Live

Without touching a script, you can set the look every player starts in: choose Tools → Mantle → Place settings and open the Mantle | Environment category. Every field is optional, and a level that leaves them alone publishes none of this.

FieldWhat it sets

Time ScaleHow fast the clock runs: 1 is the normal day, 0 stops it, 2 runs it twice as fast (0 to 60).
WeatherNatural lets it change on its own; any other setting (Clear, Partly Cloudy, Overcast, Rain, Storm, Cloudy, Foggy, Light Rain, Light Snow, Snow, Blizzard, Dust (Calm), Dust Storm) holds it there until a script changes it.

Saturation0 is black and white, 1 (the default) unchanged, 2 doubled.
