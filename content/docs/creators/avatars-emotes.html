Live An avatar is a whole body players can wear instead of Mantle's base avatars. An emote is a short animation players play from the emote wheel. A scar or tattoo is a mask players can wear on their skin. You publish all three from the Mantle Cosmetics tab. This page covers what each one needs. For signing in, the panel's fields, Check only, Upload to Mantle and screening, see Making avatar items. Players find avatars and emotes on the Avatar screen.

Avatars Live

An avatar is a MetaHuman body and face. You don't build it by hand: you pick your character under Convert a character and Mantle builds it as a MetaHuman at Mantle's default height. You don't upload any animation: Mantle animates every avatar with its own animations and players' emotes.

Converting a character

The panel's help for an avatar says: "Pick your character under Convert a character and choose Convert. Mantle builds it as a MetaHuman at Mantle's default height and checks whether the body and face are ready. Choose Use, then Upload to Mantle to add it to the catalog."

The Convert a character section shows for Avatar, Clothing and Hair. It takes a character from Fab, Sketchfab or MetaHuman Creator that the checks refuse. Nothing of yours is changed: the fixes write new assets to a Converted folder next to the character (an Epic preset outside /Game goes to /Game/Converted), and each converted part comes back to the panel, ready to preview and upload. Converting the same character again replaces what the last conversion of it wrote to that folder. Nothing else in the folder is touched.

  1. Pick the character

    Choose a Skeletal Mesh, a MetaHuman Creator character (an Epic preset or your own), a built character's Blueprint or body, or a Static Mesh. A Static Mesh has no skeleton: Convert fits it to a MetaHuman body, so it can only become an avatar. Leave the box empty to convert the Asset that is already in the panel.

  2. Look at the fixes

    Choose Convert.... Mantle shows "Looking at the character..." and then lists what it would fix: "N thing(s) to fix across N part(s), N of them here. Untick a fix to leave its problem for the checks. Nothing is written until you choose Convert; it writes to [folder]." With nothing to fix, it says "Nothing to fix in N part(s). Choose Convert to take them to the panel."

  3. Confirm what it asks

    A fix you must confirm keeps Convert off until you check it: "Tick to confirm. Convert stays off until you do." A fix the plugin can't make here is grayed out, and the line says why.

  4. Convert

    Choose Convert. It shows "Converting. Making a MetaHuman can take several minutes; the editor waits until it is done." When it ends, it says "Converted into [folder]: N fix(es) applied, N failed. N of N part(s) are ready to upload; choose Use on one to preview and upload it." A part shows "[part]: ready to upload." or "[part]: N problem(s) still stop the upload." Anything it couldn't fix is listed as "Not fixed: [part]: [problem] ([reason])", with notes about what it did or left out below that. Choose Look again to list the fixes for the character again.

  5. Use the part

    Choose Use on a part. It fills the panel with what it is, the slot, the converted mesh, its face, its section and its body, so you can preview and upload it.

What Convert does

  • Can keep only the face: if the parts of the body that read as bare skin don't cover enough of it, or clothing looks built into the body, Mantle checks the box on a line that keeps only the face and puts it on MetaHuman's own body at Mantle's default height. The line says why, for example "This character's body is covered (clothing looks built into the body), so the body underneath can't be seen". On any other body the line is there unchecked ("Optional: keep only this character's face") and you can check it. Everything below the face (clothing, armor, hair) is left out. Pick the feminine or the masculine body: "With the face kept: which body does it go on? Tick one". Convert stays off until you check one. Players then shape that body from there with the Weight, Muscle, Chest and Hips sliders.
  • Starts the body from your character's build: with the face kept, Mantle measures how wide and deep the bare skin of your character's torso is at the chest, waist and hips. It then sets MetaHuman's Fat and Muscularity body settings to match: a slim character gets a slimmer body and a wide one gets a wider body. Each setting moves at most halfway across its range. Only bare skin counts. A fully clothed character keeps MetaHuman's default body. So does one whose skin material has clothing built into it, because Mantle can't tell the body from the cloth. If Mantle can't see the build, Convert adds a note and the body stays at MetaHuman's default: "[name]: its build couldn't be seen ([reason]), so the body sliders start at MetaHuman's default." The reason is one of "none of its materials is bare skin (it is clothed or armoured), so its build can't be seen", "no bare torso could be found at its chest, waist or hips (they are under clothing)" or "[mesh]'s LOD 0 source geometry could not be read".
  • Models with no skeleton can keep their face: a Static Mesh, or a Skeletal Mesh with a single bone, gets the same three lines. The first one starts "This model's body is covered" when the body is covered, and reads "Optional: keep only this model's face" when it isn't. Mantle finds the neck and keeps everything above it as the face, so no skeleton is needed. The body settings start from its bare skin the same way. Without the face-only choice, Convert fits the whole model to a MetaHuman body, which needs bare skin from head to feet. It says so with the note "This model has no skeleton, so we'll fit it to a MetaHuman body."
  • Needs bare skin: unless you keep only the face, Convert removes the parts that don't read as bare skin (Epic gives the MetaHuman its own eyes, teeth and skin) and you confirm the rest are: "Convert won't fix a body for you: a character whose clothing is part of its body, with no skin under it, can't become a MetaHuman." You check a line to confirm the kept parts are bare skin, and you can uncheck a removal to keep a part. To convert the whole body instead of keeping the face, uncheck the face line: that needs bare skin from head to feet.
  • Poses and rebuilds the body: it poses the bare-skin body into MetaHuman's A-pose, scales it to the default height and builds a MetaHuman that matches its body and face shape, using Epic's tools. The build uses Epic's center skin tone with no underwear, because players recolor it in game. The list shows each step before it runs.
  • Uses Epic's cloud: building the MetaHuman uses Epic's tools: "Uses Epic's MetaHuman cloud (your Epic account) to make the skin and face rig." You need MetaHuman Optional Content installed (it's for Epic's skin texture synthesis) and to be signed in to Epic in MetaHuman Creator. Mantle checks both itself and never signs you in or installs anything. Without the Optional Content, the line is grayed out in the list: "MetaHuman Optional Content isn't installed (Epic's skin texture synthesis needs it): add it from Fab or the Epic launcher". Your sign-in is checked when you choose Convert, and if you're not signed in it stops with "Sign in to Epic in MetaHuman Creator first (Convert uses Epic's cloud for the skin and the face rig)".
  • Handles clothing and hair: for a Clothing or Hair part it can add simulated cloth as a plain garment (off unless you check it), refit a piece onto Mantle's neutral reference body, lower the triangles of a piece over its slot's limit, combine materials and bake them into plain textures. The first refit builds the reference body in your project: that takes a few minutes and uses Epic's cloud too. Lowering triangles needs the Skeletal Mesh Simplifier plugin; without it the line is grayed out and says so. The list line for a part Convert removes still ends "Clothing taken off here isn't offered as an item yet: that comes later." For a character in a pack with its clothing as separate pieces, or one whose clothing can be cut out, clothing does come back as Outfits: see Splitting a clothed character. When Convert rebuilds a whole MetaHuman from its face and body, its clothing and hair aren't carried through, and a note lists them.

Splitting a clothed character Live

A character from a pack often comes dressed. Convert can turn its clothes into MetaHuman Outfits you upload as clothing. It looks for the clothes in two places, in this order:

  1. Separate pieces beside the character: Convert looks in the character's folder and the folders below it for a whole bare body (nearly as tall as the character and on its skeleton) and for its clothing as separate meshes. It prefers a body that wears nothing, then one named nude or naked, then one in underwear, which Convert takes off. If it finds a bare body and separate clothing, it uses them and doesn't cut the dressed mesh.
  2. The character's own mesh: only if no separate clothing is found, Convert cuts the clothing out of the character's own material sections. It does this when you choose Use on a clothing row.

Only tops, bottoms and footwear become Outfits. A hair, glove or belt mesh isn't one, and neither is a piece that holds skin and clothing merged in one mesh.

  1. Pick the character

    With separate pieces, pick the bare body, not the dressed mesh. Convert's note names it. Without separate pieces, pick the dressed character.

  2. Convert it

    Follow Converting a character. The list shows a line such as "Clothing: [name] ([slot]); [name] ([slot]). After Convert, each piece gets a Use button that makes it a MetaHuman Outfit on this character's MetaHuman." A piece Convert can't use is listed as "[name] (can't: [reason])".

  3. Read the clothing rows

    Under "Clothing, as MetaHuman Outfits on this character's MetaHuman" each piece is a row such as "Top: [name]" with a Use button. A row that can't be made has no working Use, and the line under it says why.

  4. Choose Use on a row

    The first Use makes every piece and takes about a minute: "Making the clothing into MetaHuman Outfits on this character's MetaHuman...". The rows you can use then read "(Outfit made)".

  5. Upload the Outfit

    Use fills the panel with the Outfit as Clothing in that piece's slot, with the MetaHuman Convert made as its Authored on body. Preview it, then check and upload as in Parametric Outfits. Mantle makes its sizes from Epic's standard bodies. Choose Use on each of the other rows for the other pieces.

Mantle fits the clothes in a way that keeps their shape. It shapes the MetaHuman's skin under the clothing (and a short way past its edges) to match your character's body, and the clothes stay as you made them. The rest of the MetaHuman keeps its own shape. For how that carries over to other bodies, see How clothing fits every body.

Some pieces don't become Outfits:

  • High heels: heeled shoes aren't supported yet. Mantle makes no Outfit for them and the row shows a note. Flat shoes work.
  • Pieces with no slot: Mantle has no slot for lace sleeves, so they aren't offered. A choker or necklace isn't an Outfit either: it's a neck accessory. Convert doesn't make it for you. Make it an accessory yourself: see Accessories. The character itself still converts.

If the cut would fold the character's skin too much, Mantle doesn't offer the pieces. The skin under the clothes isn't a whole body, so the clothes would come out crushed. The character itself still converts to a MetaHuman, without that clothing. A character with a separate bare body and separate clothes doesn't hit this.

Publishing an avatar

  1. Convert it

    Follow Converting a character until you have a part that is ready to upload, and choose Use on it.

  2. Check the panel

    In the Mantle Cosmetics tab, Use has already picked Avatar under "What are you publishing?", set the slot to avatar, put the body under "Asset" and the face under "Face and sections", and filled in a name from the character. You don't need to pick any of these yourself.

  3. Move sockets if needed

    Only if accessories sit in the wrong place on your body. See Accessories and sockets.

  4. Preview it

    Choose Preview to see the avatar posed the way players' games pose it. The Mantle plugin turns IK Rig on itself, so there's nothing to enable. You don't need a UEFN mannequin in your project, because the plugin has its own. If your project has one, the plugin uses yours instead. It likes SKM_UEFN_Mannequin from the Game Animation Sample best. The avatar plays the idle (M_Neutral_Stand_Idle_Loop, from the Game Animation Sample) only when your project has its own SKM_UEFN_Mannequin. With the plugin's own mannequin, the avatar holds the A-pose and the status line says "there is no idle to play, so the avatar holds the UEFN mannequin's A-pose (to see it idle, add SKM_UEFN_Mannequin and [the idle] from the Game Animation Sample)". If the plugin can't make its own, you'll see "Your [skeleton] avatar in its reference pose: it needs [what's missing]."

  5. Check and upload

    Change the name if you like. A new item is Public, so check the rights box or choose Private: Upload to Mantle stays grayed out until you do. Use unchecks the box again, so do this after you choose it. Then choose Upload to Mantle. It checks everything first and stops at any "Problem" line, so fix those and choose it again. Choose Check only to see the list without uploading. The thumbnail is a headshot, made for you. You can choose the angle or use your own picture in the Thumbnail section: see The Thumbnail section. The upload carries the body and face geometry, the rig data and the thumbnail. The game supplies the appearance materials and textures.

Clothing and the converted body

3D clothing, hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial hair fit an avatar only if its body has Mantle's reference topology: an LOD with the same vertex layout as Mantle's reference body. A converted MetaHuman has it. Hair, eyebrows, eyelashes and facial hair also need the avatar to be a converted one with a face. Check only then shows "Done: Garment-compatible: LOD N has Mantle's reference topology, so it takes 3D clothing."

A converted body without that LOD says "not clothing-ready yet: a converted MetaHuman wears no 3D clothing for now." It still uploads and animates, with Epic's default underwear.

Players on an avatar that can't wear clothing keep their clothing and hair: those items are marked "SET ASIDE" on the Avatar screen and go back on when they switch to an avatar that fits them. Accessories and emotes work on every avatar.

The face

  • Only a MetaHuman body takes a face, and it must be a MetaHuman face on the MetaHuman face skeleton. Otherwise: "Problem: only a MetaHuman body takes a MetaHuman face."
  • A MetaHuman body ends at the neck, so its face counts toward the avatar's height. Without the face, it fails with "Problem: no head: this body has Mantle's reference topology, which ends at the neck, and no face is chosen". Convert picks the face for you.
  • The face has its own limits: 10,000 triangles, 12 material slots.
  • The face's skin takes the player's skin tone and its eyes the eye color. Players add eyelashes, eyebrows and facial hair as separate items in their own slots, which are fitted like hair.

Players with a converted avatar can also change the Height, Weight, Muscle, Chest and Hips sliders and pick scars and tattoos (see Scars and tattoos).

Height

A converted avatar is built at Mantle's default height: 165.6 cm. To publish, it has to stand within 0.5 cm of 165.6 cm. Convert aims for that and rebuilds the avatar if the first build is off. If it still comes out wrong, you'll see "WARNING: [name] did not come out at the default height" and the upload is refused. Players can change their height with the Height slider, from 95% to 105%. It scales the whole avatar. The body sliders go from -100 to 100, where 0 is the body as uploaded. After a check, the panel tells you how the avatar plays: "Plays at [N] cm." The character in play (hitboxes, camera, collision) is sized between 150 cm and 210 cm. If your avatar is outside that, the line says "Plays at [N] cm: it stands [N] cm tall, [above or below] Mantle's [N] cm limit, so its character (hitboxes, camera, collision) is clamped to [N] cm. The avatar still shows at its own height."

Accessories and sockets

Accessories attach to eight sockets that come with Mantle's copy of each supported skeleton, so your mesh doesn't need them. acc_hat and acc_face are on head, acc_neck on neck_01, acc_shoulder_l and acc_shoulder_r on clavicle_l and clavicle_r, acc_front on spine_04, acc_back on spine_03 and acc_waist on pelvis. On the UE4 Mannequin, acc_front and acc_back are on spine_02 and spine_01, because it has no spine_04. A ninth socket, hand_rSocket on hand_r, holds every tool and gun a player carries.

If your body's proportions put a hat, glasses or backpack in the wrong place, move that socket under Socket offsets ("Move where accessories and held tools attach (optional)"). The values start at the body's own socket. The tool socket stays on its bone and at scale 1, because a scaled socket scales the tool it carries. The ranges and messages are in The panel's fields. Accessories themselves are covered in Clothing, hair and accessories.

Hats are fitted to the head for you. Unless you set the acc_hat location or scale yourself, the plugin measures the head and places the hat socket on it. It measures the head only on the MetaHuman skeleton. On other skeletons it notes "Hats use the standard head size on the [skeleton] skeleton." If it can't measure the head, it says "Hats use the standard head size". Set the socket yourself if hats sit wrong.

First person

  • With a MetaHuman face, the first-person camera sits between its eyes.
  • Without a separate face, the camera sits at Mantle's standard eye height (155.9 cm at the default size), not at your mesh's eyes. Weight everything that would block the view to head or its child bones, so it hides too.
  • In first person, players don't see their own head, so they don't see the inside of their head. With a MetaHuman face, the whole face is hidden from its owner and still casts its shadow. Without one, the body's head bone is hidden. Other players still see the head.

Scars and tattoos Live

A scar or tattoo is a mask players can wear on a converted MetaHuman avatar. You paint a square mask to the MetaHuman UV layout of the body or the face, in white on black. Choose Overlay under "What are you publishing?", then the slot: scar_body, scar_face, tattoo_body or tattoo_face. The slot's name says whether it's drawn on the body or the face. Instead of painting a mask, you can place a picture on the skin: see Placing a picture.

  1. Paint the mask

    Use a square image whose size is a power of two, such as 64, 128, 256, 512 or 1024 pixels, and at most 1024 x 1024. Paint white where the scar or tattoo shows and black where it doesn't, as a grayscale image or in the red channel only. Choose Save painting guide to get an image of the UV layout to paint over: "Open this under your painting as a guide layer; paint on the area you want, then hide the guide before saving."

  2. Pick the mask

    Under "Overlay images", the mask is the Asset (a Texture 2D), or a PNG file you choose with Browse... next to Mask PNG when the Asset is empty.

  3. Add a normal map (scars only)

    A scar can add a normal map under "Scar normal map (optional)", or a PNG. Tattoos don't take one.

  4. Preview it

    Choose Preview. Mantle uses its reference body and face when your project has built them; if not, pick a converted MetaHuman body under Preview body (optional) and, for a face slot, a face under Preview face (optional). The mask is drawn on the game's shared skin material from /Game/Avatars/Appearance/Shared. If your project doesn't have it, the status line says "The shared skin material (/Game/Avatars/Appearance/Shared) is not in this project, so the mask is not drawn; the mesh shows as it is."

  5. Check and upload

    Check the rights box or choose Private, then choose Upload to Mantle. It checks first and stops at any "Problem" line. Choose Check only to see the list without uploading. Mantle screens the mask and the normal map as images.

Placing a picture instead of painting

You don't have to paint to the UV layout. You can choose a flat picture and place it on the preview body or face. Mantle bakes it into the mask players receive and, for a scar, into its normal map too. Players still get a plain mask, so nothing changes for them. A face scar lands on the face of MetaHuman Creator's template, and a scar's relief reads as raised.

  1. Choose the slot

    Choose Overlay under "What are you publishing?", then the slot. A body slot places on the body and a face slot on the face.

  2. Switch on placing

    Check "Place a picture on the body or face (instead of painting a mask)". The Mask PNG and scar normal map boxes hide while it's checked. Uncheck it to paint instead.

  3. Pick the picture

    Under Picture, choose a Texture 2D, or choose Browse... next to "or a picture PNG" to pick a PNG file. White (or the opaque part) of the picture shows. Black (or the clear part) doesn't.

  4. Pick the skin (optional)

    The picture is placed on MetaHuman Creator's template body or face. If your project doesn't have it, Mantle uses its own reference body or face. To use another converted MetaHuman, pick it under Preview body (optional) or Preview face (optional).

  5. Place it

    The panel says: "Choose a flat picture, then click the skin where it goes. Drag the arrows to move it (W), turn it (E) or size it (R). White (or the opaque part) of the picture shows; black (or the clear part) does not. Preview shows the result; upload bakes it into the mask players receive." Click the skin in the small viewport, then drag the arrows. Choose Put it on the chest / face to start again, or Frame to bring the picture back into view.

  6. Fine-tune it

    Four boxes set the picture. Width on the skin (cm) is how wide it is. How far it reaches in and out (cm) is how far from the skin it still shows. Fade at the reach's edge softens that edge. Scar relief sets how raised a scar looks, and it shows for scars only.

  7. Preview and upload

    Choose Preview to see the result. Then check the rights box or choose Private, and choose Upload to Mantle, as in the steps above. Judge a scar's relief in Preview.

Limits of placing a picture
  • A flat picture can't wrap around an arm or a leg. On a tilted limb it breaks into pieces. Turn the arrows (E) until the picture lies flatter on the skin, or paint the mask instead.
  • Small marks look coarse. The mask is always 1024 x 1024 across the whole body, so a mark about 14 cm wide is only about 70 pixels wide.
  • A scar's relief follows the picture's brightness. Check it in Preview.

Emotes Live

An emote is an Anim Sequence. Mantle plays it on whichever avatar the player wears, so one emote works on every avatar alike. Animate it on the UEFN mannequin skeleton (SK_UEFN_Mannequin), or on a MetaHuman body, the UE5 Mannequin (SK_Mannequin) or the UE4 Mannequin (SK_UE4_Mannequin): Mantle identifies which one you used from its bones and, for the latter three, retargets it onto the UEFN mannequin automatically when you upload. There's nothing to set for the retarget: pick your Anim Sequence under Asset and upload it as usual.

Publishing an emote

  1. Make the animation

    Animate on SK_UEFN_Mannequin, a MetaHuman body, the UE5 Mannequin or the UE4 Mannequin, or retarget an existing animation onto one of them (right-click it and choose Retarget Animations) and use the retargeted copy.

  2. Keep it in place

    Keep the root bone within 50 cm of where it starts, and animate pelvis. See Emote rules.

  3. Pick Emote

    In the Mantle Cosmetics tab, pick Emote under "What are you publishing?". The slot is emote. Under "Asset", choose the Anim Sequence.

  4. Say which character it was made on (optional)

    Mantle finds the character itself. Pick one under "Animated on" only if the animation has no preview mesh and the mesh Mantle finds isn't the character you animated on. A MetaHuman, UE5 Mannequin or UE4 Mannequin clip is retargeted from this body's proportions.

  5. Choose how it plays

    Under "Playback", "Loop until the player moves" is on by default. Turn it off for an emote that plays once, like a bow. See Looping.

  6. Check and upload

    Check the rights box or choose Private, then choose Upload to Mantle. It checks first and stops at any "Problem" line. Choose Check only to see the list without uploading. The panel has no Preview for emotes. If your clip is on a MetaHuman, UE5 Mannequin or UE4 Mannequin skeleton, Files and details notes that it was retargeted onto the UEFN mannequin.

Looping

The "Playback" section of the Mantle Cosmetics tab has one checkbox: "Loop until the player moves". It's on by default.

  • On: without a loop_start notify, the whole clip loops, and its end blends into its first frame so the jump back is seamless. The emote keeps playing until the player moves, jumps or falls, sits down, gets hit, dies, ragdolls or plays another emote.
  • Off: the emote plays once and blends out when it reaches the end.

To loop only part of a clip, add an Anim Notify named loop_start where the loop should begin. With Loop on, the emote plays to the end, then loops from the notify's time. With Loop off, Mantle leaves the notify out. Files and details tells you when it adds a loop or leaves one out.

Emotes you uploaded before this setting existed play once, unless they already had a loop_start notify. To make one loop, upload it again with Loop on.

How players use emotes

  • Players get emotes from the catalog and wear up to 8 at a time. They play them from the emote wheel: hold B or right bumper while not holding a weapon, point, and let go. See Emotes for players.
  • The emote blends in and out smoothly. Everyone in the world sees it at the same point in the clip, even if they join partway through.
  • A looping emote stops when the player moves, jumps or falls, sits down, gets hit, dies, ragdolls or plays another emote. It can't start while they're moving, in the air, seated or dead, or within half a second of their last emote.
  • World scripts can play one of a player's equipped emotes with Avatar:PlayEmote(itemId). See the API reference.
