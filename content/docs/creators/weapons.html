You can turn a 3D model into a weapon players can hold and use. You pick a weapon type, choose your model, check its size and direction, place the hand and action points, set the numbers, and add parts and sounds if you like. Then you create the weapon, try it and publish it. You place it in a level with a Mantle Weapon Spawn.

What you can build Live

There are 17 weapon types: 8 guns, 3 melee weapons, a spear, a staff, a bow, a shield, a throwable and a consumable. Each one has its own grip, animations and attacks (a consumable is used up instead of fired). The weapon types are the only starting points: there are no other ready-made presets. There's no crossbow, wand or dual wield.

Where to find it Live

In the Unreal Editor, choose Tools → Mantle → Build a weapon... The Build a weapon tab opens with eight steps. Use Back and Next. On the last step the button says Create weapon. To change a weapon you made earlier, use Open a saved weapon to edit in step 1. It lists the weapons in your project's MantleWeapons folder.

Made a weapon a while ago?

Earlier versions of the builder could lose some of a model's triangles. Open the weapon and press Create weapon again to rebuild it.

Building a weapon Live

  1. What kind of weapon?

    Pick the closest card. This decides how the character holds your weapon. Type a name in Name your weapon: players see it. You can't go on without one. If you already have a weapon with that name, the new one is saved as "Name 2", "Name 3" and so on, and the window tells you. A name is saved as a folder name: spaces and dashes become underscores, and it's cut to 60 characters.

  2. Choose the 3D model

    Pick a Static Mesh or a Skeletal Mesh from your project, or drag one in. A Skeletal Mesh is read in its reference pose. Every weapon needs a model, fist weapons too: pick the knuckles, claws or gauntlet.

  3. Size and direction

    The window guesses which way the weapon points and how big it should be, and tells you in a sentence ("The longest side of this weapon model is about 85 cm."). If the model looks like it was made in meters, inches or millimeters, it's scaled for you. A grey figure shows the weapon next to a character. Fix mistakes with Model size (1 = as imported) (0.01 to 100), Reset size, Flip front and back and Turn upside down. For a gun, if the length is far from normal for the type, an orange message says so, and it may offer a Switch to Pistol or Switch to Rifle button.

  4. Place hands and action points

    Pick a point in the list, then drag it in the picture onto the right spot on your model. Hand points go in the middle of the palm. A point marked "(optional)" can be skipped. Reset this point and Reset all points put points back where the window guessed them. Next stays off until every required point is placed. The window's guesses are only a start, so check each one. A posed figure holds your weapon so you can see if the hands reach. A gun's notes show under the picture in orange (see Messages you may see). A gun, staff or bow also has a Show aiming box that shows the player's eye and line of sight.

  5. Attack and effect

    Every setting starts with a sensible value for the type. Only the settings that apply to your type show. Each number stays inside its allowed range. Go back to the usual attack numbers resets damage, attacks per second, rounds, reload time, range, shot spread and recoil. It leaves the other settings alone. See Settings and their ranges.

  6. Parts and sound

    Optional. Add a magazine that moves while you reload, fixed model details and effects, and your own attack sound. See Parts.

  7. Thumbnail

    The picture of your weapon in the library. "Mantle takes one for you; you can choose the angle or use your own picture." Automatic shows your model on its own. Consumables get a picture too. See The Thumbnail section for the three choices. Mantle remembers the choice for each weapon by its name.

  8. Create

    Check the summary, then press Create weapon. It says "Your weapon is ready: [name]." and "Your weapon's files are in MantleWeapons/[name].", and the files appear in Content/MantleWeapons/[name] in your project. Then you can press Test locally to hold the weapon in the game, or publish it (see Publishing a weapon). If you change anything after Create weapon, both buttons switch off until you press Create weapon again. The Thumbnail step is the exception: a new choice there is saved at once and used the next time you publish, so you don't need to create the weapon again.

Consumables Live

A consumable is a potion, a medkit or a snack. The character holds it in one hand and uses it up. Step 5 shows these settings instead of the attack ones:

  • How it is used: "Eat or drink (bottle to the lips)" (the usual) plays the drinking animation. "Use (lift the hand and crush; medkits)" plays a lift-and-crush animation, a good fit for a medkit.
  • What it does: "Heal" (the usual) gives health. "Nothing of its own (a script does it)" gives none: a script in your place does the rest. See Backpack in the API reference for how a script hears that someone used one.
  • Health given: 1 to 1,000 (usually 50), up to the character's maximum health. It only shows when What it does is "Heal".
  • Spread over (seconds): 0 to 60 (usually 0). 0 gives all the health at once. A higher number spreads it evenly over that many seconds. It only shows for "Heal".
  • Uses: 1 to 100 (usually 1). The consumable is gone after its last use.
  • Choose how long one use takes: off (the usual), a use takes as long as the animation, 2.5 seconds. Tick it to set Use time (seconds), 0.5 to 10. The animation plays faster or slower to fit.
  • Wait between uses (seconds): 0 to 600 (usually 0, no wait).
  • Can walk while using: ticked by default. Untick it and the character stands still until the use ends.

Step 4 has one optional point, Grip: the middle of the palm where the hand holds the item. A consumable has no moving parts, extra details or custom sounds, so step 6 only says "A consumable has no moving parts, extra details or custom sounds. Go on to Create." The Thumbnail step comes next. Players see how to use one in Tools, backpack and weapons.

Parts In progress

Step 6 has two lists, each with up to 16 entries:

  • Moving parts: press Add moving part, then pick a Part type, a model, a position, a rotation and a size. Show during: lists the actions where the part shows. Leave Use this hold's usual loading or drawing timing ticked unless it should show during different actions. Today only the Magazine part works, and only the first one counts. It's there on the pistol, rifle, SMG, marksman rifle, sniper rifle and machine gun. Don't add Round or shell or Arrow parts yet: a weapon with one won't load in the game. To change how a flying round or an arrow looks, use Round or arrow model in step 5. Other types say "This weapon type has no separate magazine, round or arrow parts."
  • Fixed details and effects: press Add model detail or Add built-in effect. A detail has a model, an effect or both. Give each one a different Detail name with only letters, numbers and underscores, such as "Scope_Ring". Attach at picks the weapon root or one of your placed points. Start hidden makes the detail start out of sight.

Sounds: Fire or attack sound takes a Sound Wave. Quieter attack sound plays when you tick Use the quieter sound while suppressed. Leave a sound empty to use the usual one for the type.

Publishing a weapon Live

On the last step, Publish to your library adds the weapon to your Mantle library. Pick "Public: other creators can find it and use it in their places" or "Private: only you can use it". A public weapon needs you to tick "I own this content or its licence permits public redistribution on Mantle". See Who can use your items for what Public and Private mean, and for how a place uses a weapon from its Storage. Publishing again after a change adds a new version. Press Create weapon first, and again after any change except a new Thumbnail choice.

The weapon's library card shows the picture from the Thumbnail step once Mantle has approved it. A consumable gets one too.

The weapon's model, extra parts and sounds go up with it and follow the same limits as other meshes and sounds in a place:

  • Weapon model: up to 100,000 triangles, 8 material slots and 256 bones.
  • Moving parts: a moving part's model is built into the weapon model, so it counts toward the weapon model's limits above.
  • Detail and round models: static meshes up to 500,000 triangles and 8 material slots. Skeletal ones follow the weapon model's limits.
  • Textures and sounds: see Custom meshes and materials. An attack sound is a WAV file, mono or stereo, 22,050 to 48,000 Hz, up to 60 seconds and 12 MiB.

Putting it in a place Live

Choose Tools → Mantle → Add Weapon Spawn and pick your weapon in its Weapon list, or use a Storage Item for a weapon from your place's storage. See Weapon spawns for every setting, the 32-per-place limit and what "Check this place" reports.
