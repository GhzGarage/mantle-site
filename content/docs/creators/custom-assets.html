Live You can build a world from static meshes, materials and textures in your own project, whether you made them yourself or they came from a Fab pack. You never upload them by hand: when you publish, the plugin finds every mesh and material the level uses that isn't in Mantle's built-in library, prepares it for Mantle and uploads anything new.

Before you start Live

  • You need the Mantle plugin and to be signed in with a creator token.
  • Static Mesh Actors, Blueprint prefabs, skeletal meshes, landscape, foliage and your own vehicles all publish. See What you can place.
  • Sound waves from your project publish too. See Sounds.

Getting your assets into a world Live

  1. Bring the assets into your project

    Add the Fab pack to your project, or import your own meshes and textures, as you normally would in Unreal. Nothing about them needs to change for Mantle.

  2. Place them in the level

    Drag each Static Mesh into the level so it becomes a Static Mesh Actor. If a pack gives you Blueprint prefabs, place them as they are: their meshes, lights and collision publish as parts (see Blueprint prefabs). You can change materials on the actor's slots as usual; a slot with no material uses Unreal's default material.

  3. Set collision and shadows on the actor

    The actor's Collision Enabled decides how players meet it: No Collision publishes as none, Query Only as overlap, and anything else as blocking. Cast Shadow is kept too.

  4. Save your assets

    Save the level and any meshes or materials you changed. Unsaved assets still publish, but they're prepared again on every publish, which is slower. See The conversion cache.

  5. Run Check this place

    Choose Tools → Mantle → Check this place. It checks every mesh and material from your project once, however many actors use it. Errors must be fixed before you can publish. Warnings tell you what will look different in Mantle, but don't stop anything. See Messages and how to fix them.

  6. Publish

    Choose Tools → Mantle → Publish (or Save to Mantle for a private draft). The Mantle Publish tab opens and shows each step. You can keep working while it runs.

What players see Live

Meshes Live

  • Levels of detail: the mesh's own LOD1 and LOD2 are published with the screen sizes set in the editor, so players far away see the lighter versions. A LOD with more triangles than the one before it, or past the limits above, is left off with a warning: "Mesh '…': LOD[N] was left off because …; players see the LODs before it at every distance.". If the LODs make the file bigger than 64 MB, only LOD0 is published, with a warning. UV channel 1 and painted vertex colors are published too.
  • Bad LOD screen sizes: if your LOD screen sizes don't each go down, or LOD0's is above 1, Mantle fixes them in its copy and warns you: "Mesh '…': its LOD screen sizes (…) were not each below the one before, so they were published as (…)." Your mesh isn't changed. To use your own sizes, set each LOD's screen size smaller than the one before.
  • Draw distance: players stop seeing small things when they're far away. To choose the distance yourself, set Desired Max Draw Distance on the mesh you placed in the level (in its Details panel). Turn on Never Distance Cull there to always show it. Foliage and other instanced meshes use their Instance End Cull Distance. Otherwise Mantle picks a distance from the mesh's size, as the table shows. A distance must be above 0 and at most 10 km (1,000,000 cm). A longer one is ignored, and players always see the mesh.
  • Sky domes: a huge dome or sphere mesh with a sky material is left out, because Mantle supplies its own sky. Check this place warns you: "a sky mesh; Mantle supplies its own sky, so it isn't published." (or, for a Blueprint with a sky mesh, "has a sky mesh ('…'); Mantle supplies its own sky, so it isn't published."). The rest of the actor still publishes.
  • Mesh actors with no mesh: a Static Mesh Actor with no mesh assigned is left out of the place instead of stopping the publish. Check this place warns you: "has no mesh assigned, so there is nothing to show and it is left out of the place." Pick a mesh for it to keep it.
  • Over the triangle or vertex limit: Mantle doesn't change your mesh. It publishes the mesh's first lower LOD that fits (and draws with the same material slots), which then becomes its LOD0, and the LODs after it move up. If no LOD fits, it reduces a copy of the mesh to about 90% of the limit. The copy is published as a single LOD, so players see it at every distance. Either way, Check this place warns you. If the mesh can't be reduced, that's an error, and the message says why.
  • Nanite: players see a Nanite mesh's fallback mesh. If it looks too coarse, raise Fallback Triangle Percent in the mesh's Nanite settings. If the fallback is over the triangle limit, Mantle reduces it as above, or you can lower the percent.
  • Collision: a mesh's simple collision (boxes, spheres, capsules and convex hulls) is published, unless its Collision Complexity is Use Complex As Simple. A mesh without simple collision, or with more than the limits above, collides using its own triangles. Tapered capsules, level sets and shapes with no volume are left out, with a warning.

Materials Live

When you publish, Mantle converts each material to work in the game. This works for any material, including material instances and graphs built from material functions.

  • Kept: base color, normal, roughness, metallic, ambient occlusion and emissive (with its strength, up to 100).
  • Blend modes: Opaque, Masked (with its opacity mask clip value) and Translucent. Additive blend (below) is kept; other blend modes become ordinary translucency.
  • Two Sided is kept.
  • Unlit materials become a glowing material that lighting still touches.
  • Per-texture tiling, offset and rotation, normal and occlusion strength, ambient occlusion on UV channel 1, and vertex color multiplying the base color.
  • Car paint: an opaque, one-sided Clear Coat material publishes as Mantle's car paint, a clear coat over its base color, with optional metallic flakes (give it FlakeDensity, FlakeSize or FlakeTint parameters). "Check this place" notes it: "it is a Clear Coat material, so it publishes as Mantle's car-paint shading".
  • Scrolling textures: a Panner feeding a Texture Sample's UVs keeps scrolling in game at the same speed. The sample must go straight into Base Color, Normal or Emissive (or the ORM group). On a masked or translucent material, the opacity has to be that same sample's alpha.
  • Swaying grass and foliage: a SimpleGrassWind node on World Position Offset sways in game instead of standing still. Give it constant Wind Intensity and Wind Speed, and a Wind Weight of Vertex Color's red, 1 - V of the first UV, or a constant 1. It stops swaying past 50 m, and doesn't work on translucent materials.
  • World-aligned textures: a WorldAlignedTexture (or WorldAlignedNormal) node on Base Color, Normal or the ORM group keeps its pattern running on unbroken across neighboring meshes. Give it one constant tile size and sharpness. It works on opaque materials only.
  • Vertex-painted layer blends: a Lerp between two plain Texture Samples by one Vertex Color channel, on Base Color, keeps the painted layer in game. Normal and the ORM group can blend the same way. Opaque materials only.
  • Additive materials: an Additive blend publishes as an additive material, which shows only its glow. The glow is the emissive (or, for an unlit material, its color), scaled by a constant opacity. The base color and its vertex-color tint aren't kept.
  • Glass: an unlit translucent material that draws the scene behind it (Scene Color on its emissive path) publishes as see-through tinted glass.
  • Refraction: glass that bends light publishes as smooth, tinted glass. The scene behind it isn't bent. On an opaque or masked material, the index of refraction is dropped, and you see "Material '…': its ior part is dropped."
  • Water: a water material (Single Layer Water, or a see-through material that fades with depth) publishes as Mantle's water. It keeps the surface color, roughness and up to two moving normal maps. Its color, roughness, metallic and occlusion textures, glow, metallic look and waves are dropped, with a warning. Mantle's water is a flat surface.
  • Foliage shading: a masked, two-sided material built as foliage keeps its tint, strength and vertex-color weight.

Mantle only keeps scrolling, swaying, world-aligned and layer-blend effects when they're built the simple, common way described above. Anything built differently, even if it looks the same in the material editor, publishes without the effect. A material keeps only one of swaying, world-aligned and layer blend: swaying comes first, then world-aligned. None of them are kept on car paint. On an unlit material, only swaying and a scrolling glow are kept.

Some effects can't be kept. Check this place names each one as a warning, and the material still publishes without it. The rows in italics are the exceptions: when the effect is built the simple way above, it carries across.

Textures Live

  • Textures are at most 2048 on the longest side. Larger ones are scaled down when you publish, keeping their shape. Detail finer than that is lost.
  • Each texture file must fit in 8 MB. If one is too big, it's compressed harder or scaled down, with a warning.
  • Every texture is screened before a place can go live with it.
  • If one image is used for two different jobs, such as color in one material and a normal map in another, one of them may be left out. See the upload messages.

The conversion cache Live

The plugin keeps what it has prepared in your project's Saved/Mantle/AssetCache folder, so a later publish only prepares what changed.

  • Only saved assets are kept. An asset with unsaved changes is prepared every time until you save it.
  • A plugin update or a different engine version may convert everything again once.
  • It's safe to delete the folder. Everything is simply converted again on the next publish.

Updating or replacing an asset Live

  • To change an asset, edit it in Unreal, save it and publish again. Only what changed is uploaded.
  • To replace an asset, swap it in the level (or on the actor's material slot) and publish again. The new one is uploaded like any other.
  • Servers already running keep the revision they started with, so players in them see the old version until the server empties. See Revisions.
  • If you edit the level while a publish is running, the plugin prepares and uploads what's new before saving. If the level keeps changing, it stops with "The level kept changing while it was being published. Pause editing for a moment and choose Publish again; nothing is uploaded twice."

If a file is rejected or removed Live

  • Rejected in screening or review: the place can't go live with it. Replace the texture (or the material or mesh that uses it), or remove what uses it, then publish again.
  • Waiting for a moderator: nothing goes live until it's approved. Choose Publish again once it is. Nothing is uploaded twice.
  • Removed by a moderator: moderators can remove a file from your account, usually after a license complaint (for example a Fab pack you don't have the rights to). After that it can't be uploaded or used again, and your world's next publish is stopped until you replace it or remove what uses it.
Only use assets you have the rights to

Everything you upload is tied to your account. Use your own work, or Fab and marketplace content whose license lets you use it in your game.

Crediting assets Live

If an asset's license asks for credit, such as Creative Commons BY, add it in Project Settings → Plugins → Mantle Credits. Each rule names a content folder, such as /Game/Fab/Crates, and the Attribution players are shown for everything under it: plain text, one line, at most 500 characters, for example the title, author, source and license. The deepest matching folder wins. One asset can override its folder with the MantleAttribution metadata tag on its package (an empty tag means no credit). Credits show on the world page of every place that uses the asset. Nothing is credited unless a rule or tag says so, and Mantle never guesses a license. The rules are saved with the project, so everyone working on it shares them. A credit over 500 characters stops the publish before anything is uploaded: "The credit for '…' is longer than the 500 characters Mantle shows. Shorten its Credits rule (Project Settings > Plugins > Mantle Credits) or its MantleAttribution tag, then Publish again."

Upload limits Live

Each account can upload up to 5,000 files (meshes, materials, textures, sounds, skeletal meshes, animation clips, landscapes, vehicles and weapons) and 2 GiB per hour. Files your account already has on Mantle aren't sent again, so they don't count. A big pack's first publish can reach the limit. When it does, Publish pauses by itself and shows "Mantle's hourly upload limit is reached. Uploading continues by itself at HH:MM (in about N minutes); you can keep working meanwhile." If the wait would be longer than about an hour, it stops instead and tells you when to try again. Everything uploaded so far is kept. See Official hosting for the other limits.
