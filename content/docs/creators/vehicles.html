Coming to the Mantle plugin

The vehicle converter isn't in the Mantle plugin yet. This guide describes how it will work, so you can get a car ready now. Until it arrives, a vehicle placed in your level gets the "Check this place" warning "is a placed [type], which Mantle does not publish." and is left out when you publish.

How it works Planned

Most cars you can make or buy for Unreal are built on Epic's Chaos Vehicles plugin. The converter takes a Chaos Vehicles car and turns it into a Mantle vehicle that:

  • Looks the same: the same skeletal mesh, animation and control rig, with the same meshes, glass and lights in the same places.
  • Drives as close to the same as possible: the car's engine, gearbox, suspension, tyre, brake, steering and camera settings come across with it.

Players get in with F / Y and drive it with the usual vehicle controls.

What you can convert

  • A Wheeled Vehicle Pawn Blueprint that uses the Chaos Wheeled Vehicle Movement component. Unreal's Vehicle template cars are built this way.
  • A skeletal mesh with a bone at the centre of each wheel, a physics asset for the body, and an animation blueprint that uses Wheel Controller nodes to spin and steer the wheels.

Vehicles built some other way, such as ones with their own C++ movement code, can't be converted.

Getting a car ready

  1. Check it drives in its own project

    Drive the car in the project it came from first. The converter copies its settings as they are, so tune it there until it handles the way you want.

  2. Keep its content together

    The converter uses everything the car's Blueprint points to: the skeletal mesh, skeleton, physics asset, animation blueprint, control rig, and any static meshes, materials and textures attached to it. It doesn't use the car's own Blueprint graph or C++ classes, so gameplay logic in those doesn't come across.

  3. Mark the driver's seat

    Give the seat a material slot whose name contains Seat, and the converter places the driver there. If your car has no such slot, you'll set the driver's position yourself.

  4. Check the wheel bones

    Each wheel needs its own bone at the wheel's centre. It doesn't matter which way the wheel bones face.

What you get

The converter gives you a vehicle you can drag into your level, plus a vehicle definition holding its settings. You can edit the definition afterwards, including the driver's seat position. Your original car isn't changed.

What's different

  • Handling: the converted car drives close to the original rather than identically. Test drive it and adjust the definition if you need to.
  • Not carried across: a few advanced settings, such as a front-to-rear torque split other than 50/50, don't come across.

When a conversion stops

ProblemWhat to do
The Blueprint isn't a Chaos Vehicles Wheeled Vehicle PawnOnly Chaos Vehicles cars can be converted. See What you can convert.
Some of the car's content is missingBring every asset the car uses into your project, not just the Blueprint.
The converted animation blueprint doesn't compileMake sure the original animation blueprint compiles and drives the wheels with Wheel Controller nodes.
No driver's seat foundName the seat's material slot with Seat, or set the driver's position yourself.
